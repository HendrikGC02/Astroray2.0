# 0004 — Integrator: transport algorithms, lights, sampling, light tracing and diagnostics
Status: proposed (2026-11-14), revised after independent review (2026-11-14), reconciled with RULINGS.md (2026-11-14)

## Context

The integrator turns the synced scene (geometry, OpenPBR records, media, lights, world) into film samples. The owner's decisions bind it: correctness against physics, not Cycles (0000 #1); one kernel source for CPU and GPU (#2); full breadth in the first release, including sky, world and lights (#7); a viewport targeting 30 updates/s at full resolution with 24 the floor, and a first frame comparable to Cycles (#9, #10, #21, #23); heat maps of sample count, bounce count and similar statistics (#15); diffraction gratings in the first release, with the light-tracing path that makes them useful designed in from the first milestone (#17); fluorescence for all its use cases (#19).

The spine fixes the frame: wavefront stages written once; surface shading as one fused BSDF+NEE kernel under `__launch_bounds__` (spike A, MEASURED); structurally different work, including the light-tracing pass and the extension-lobe stage, gets its own kernel and queue; every kernel within 0002's budgets; 4 stored, rewritable transport wavelengths; a counter-based sampler with no stored state; colour-aware adaptive sampling; per-pixel moments and statistics buffers. Research file 04 (R1-R12) is the starting design. This record turns it into concrete algorithms on 0002's pools, queues and kernel list, 0003's path state and sampler, and 0008's film.

Unidirectional path tracing cannot render the owner's named effects. A grating order and a smooth dispersive refraction are delta in direction for each wavelength. Point, spot, zero-angle sun and collimated area lights are delta in position or direction. So a path light → delta chain → diffuse surface → camera (Heckbert notation L S+ D E) has zero probability from the camera side, and with small non-delta lights it shows up as fireflies. v1 tried camera-side manifold methods on prisms and failed (no focusing on flat faces, chaotic Newton basins); forward photon transport is what shipped (`v1-lessons/research/transport.md`). The review of research file 17 records the same gap for gratings, CD scenes and Mie rainbows.

v1 also left lessons that constrain this record: one-sided MIS biased direct light; light-tree seams and a sun starved by mesh emitters; a colour-blind adaptive metric that left a blue variance floor; clamps that capped real caustic energy; light-energy conventions found one bug at a time; a general stochastic photon loop on a solid prism that looked like chromatic noise and passed metric gates (`v1-lessons/research/lights.md`, `transport.md`, `physics-and-rendering.md`, `testing-and-process.md`).

## Decision

**Challenges to the spine: none.** Two clarifications. (a) The path state carries no radiance: every contribution goes to 0008's per-sample buffer (ruling 1), so shadow items stay self-contained as 0002 requires. (b) The light-tracing pass uses 0002's `generate_light` and `shade_light_path` kernels plus a light variant of each extension identity, so extension code stays out of the common light-path kernel (spine §2).

**Owned here:** transport algorithms, the MIS form, light sampling, sky, and light-tracing pass behaviour. **Cited, not restated:** the C ABI and per-render report schema, codes, deduplication and polling (0001); kernel and OptiX program list, queues, budgets and the instance visibility mask (0002); path state and shadow item layouts, packet operations, flag and termination enums, sampler and dimension map (0003); material record and VM (0005); medium interface (0006); render control, sync, edit classification, VRAM budget and first-frame gate (0007); film, per-sample buffer and resolve, splats, passes (0008); every time budget (0009).

Number labels: MEASURED (spike or source measurement cited), SOURCE (a constant taken from a cited implementation), DERIVED (computed from cited numbers), PROVISIONAL (a value to confirm by the named measurement).

### A. Transport core

- **D1. Estimator and MIS.** Unidirectional wavefront path tracing. At every non-delta vertex (surface or medium scatter): one light sample (NEE) plus one BSDF or phase sample to continue. This is Veach's multi-sample MIS model with one sample per strategy, weighted by the balance heuristic in pbrt-v4 `VolPathIntegrator`'s rescaled-probability form: beta, r_u and r_l are 4-wide spectra, so the weights also combine wavelengths (Veach and Guibas 1995; pbr-book 4e §14.2; research 04 R6). It is the only MIS form in the engine (ruling 5), for surfaces, media and every record.
  - Delta lights: NEE weight 1; BSDF-strategy pdf 0.
  - Emitter hit by a BSDF ray: weighted with `LightSampler::pmf(ctx_prev, light) * light.pdf_li(ctx_prev, wi)`, using the *previous* vertex's context, the same selection pdf and the same solid-angle measure as NEE (v1 seam fix). A path whose last segment was curved (0003 decision 30's `LAST_SEGMENT_CURVED`) uses 0006 D28's connection gate and the straight NEE pdf from its last vertex, kept in the geodesic record (0006 D28, D41).
  - Media: NEE from a scatter vertex is weighted as at a surface with the phase pdf in place of the BSDF pdf. 0006's two distance strategies (delta tracking and equiangular, ½ each) are a one-sample balance-heuristic combination: the estimate divides by the mixture pdf ½p_dt + ½p_eq, evaluated per lane through r_u with the path's hero lane (0003, ruling 4).
  - Convention: `f` excludes the cosine. The integrator multiplies |cos θ_i| against the shading normal exactly once.
- **D2. Russian roulette.** Rule from pbrt-v4 VolPath: at depth ≥ max(2, Blender min bounces), q = max(0, 1 − max_λ(beta·η_scale / avg(r_u))); survivors are weighted 1/(1−q) (Arvo and Kirk 1990). The max over lanes is lane-symmetric (0003, hero lane). η_scale undoes the radiance η² so refraction does not trigger termination. Light paths apply the same rule to power. Its uniform comes from 0003's dimension map.
- **D3. Bounce semantics.** Per-type limits (total, diffuse, glossy, transmission, volume, transparent) are honoured as Blender defines them (`v1-lessons/research/transport.md`): an opacity pass-through is a null event that advances only the transparent counter and keeps the ray direction; a lamp crossing is a pass-through, not a vertex (D8); at the cap, a transmission sample keeps its weight. These counters are Blender's limits only; the sampler's event index is separate (D14). **Opacity null event** (ruling 87; built in `shade_surface`, 0002 D11): the hit first adds its emission, which already carries the opacity (0005 item 38: a × Le); the decision of 0005 item 38 then either takes the surface or makes the null event, which advances the transparent counter and the event index only, keeps direction, flags, beta, r_u, r_l and the previous vertex, and re-queues `intersect_closest` from just past the hit with prev_dist grown by the distance the origin moved. A path that arrives with the transparent counter at its limit and draws the null event ends `TRANSPARENT_LIMIT` after its emission instead of passing (Blender: the next surface after the limit emits and terminates; here only a hit that would pass ends, where Cycles ends any surface after the limit, and a limit of 0 ends the first pass-through, where Cycles' counter would still let one pass). The decision comes before the primary-hit AOVs and the total-bounce test, so a pass-through at the total limit still passes and the AOVs name the first surface the path does not pass. Each path end records a termination reason (D28).
- **D4. Shadow stage.**
  - The shade stage computes the unoccluded contribution (light sample × f × |cos| × MIS weight) and emits a self-contained shadow item (0002): ray, tmax, contribution at the 4 lanes, the sample's `sample_id` (D5) and light-group id (D27), plus the media fields 0003 lists; its pass category (D27) goes to 0003 decision 20's shadow-pass side buffer at the item's shadow slot. It never reads or writes its creating path.
  - The shadow stage terminates on the first opaque hit; null (opacity) surfaces pass, evaluated in the shadow stage with Blender's Is Shadow Ray set, within the transparent-bounce budget; media contribute transmittance (0006). A surface whose shadow-ray evaluation has a non-null BSDF blocks, smooth glass included; the partition relies on this (D19).
  - Delivery adds the contribution to the sample's slot (D5) and to enabled light-group and light-path planes (D27).
- **D5. Accumulation.** Every contribution of a camera sample (emission found by the path, a lamp crossing, a shadow delivery, including deliveries after the path has ended or its slot was refilled) is added to the sample's slot in 0008's per-sample buffer; at path end the path writes its final flags (`SECONDARIES_TERMINATED` and the invalid bit, 0008 decision 3) into the slot. The slot is the sample's rank in the pass, its `sample_id` (0003 decision 19; ruling 37), and travels in every shadow item. 0002 drains the pass; 0008 resolves the buffer into the film's moments. 0003's `film_weights` applies to the whole sample at resolve, as in pbrt. The buffer, its precision, its resolve and its size are 0008's (ruling 1); its memory is in 0007's budget table.

### B. Lights

- **D6. Light types and energy conventions.** Internal quantities are spectral and in physical units. ŝ(λ) is the light's normalised emission spectrum from colour, temperature or a table (0003 upsampling as illuminant). The scale from Blender's watts is 0003 decision 11's unit anchor (factor 1; ruling 35); this table fixes the geometry, taken from v1's Cycles-semantics findings.

| Type | Blender input | Internal quantity | NEE sampling | Hit by BSDF rays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Point, radius 0 | power P | intensity I = P/(4π)·ŝ | delta, pdf 1 | no |
| Point/sphere, radius r > 0 | P | radiance L = P/(4π²r²)·ŝ (area-consistent) | visible-cone sampling (pbrt-v4 `Sphere::Sample`) | yes |
| Spot | P, angle, blend | as point or sphere; × smoothstep blend in cosine space; not renormalised to the cone | as point/sphere | if r > 0 |
| Area: rectangle, square, disk, ellipse | P, spread | L = P/(πA)·ŝ, one-sided; spread attenuation `max(tan h − tan a, 0)·N`, N = 1/(tan h − h) (Cycles soft-box, power-preserving); spread → 0 is collimated | rectangles: spherical rectangle (Ureña, Fajardo, King 2013, as in Cycles `light/area.h`); disk and ellipse: area sampling | yes |
| Sun, angle θ | strength E [W/m²] | irradiance E·ŝ, independent of θ; radiance E/Ω inside the cone, Ω = 2π(1 − cos θ/2); θ = 0 is delta | uniform cone | yes if θ > 0 |
| Emissive mesh | material emission | OpenPBR emission radiance (0005) | D9 | yes |
| World: HDRI, sky, constant | world node program | radiance | D11, D12 | escaped rays |

  - A BLACKBODY lamp colour is ŝ = Planck(T)/Y(Planck(T)) times the colour's scale, so the lamp's display luminance is P times that scale at any temperature (0003 decision 11; the rgb field is ignored), and a temperature whose Planck radiance underflows over the whole observer support (a few kelvin) keeps T, skips the normalisation, emits what Planck gives and raises one report entry saying it has no visible radiance (ruling 87).
  - IES profiles are an angular multiplier on intensity with absolute candela scale, converted to the engine's units through 0003 decision 11's anchor (ruling 35). The light's power estimate integrates the profile.
  - Blender's per-light ray-visibility flags apply to lamp primitives through 0002's instance visibility mask (D8) and are honoured by light paths (D19).
- **D7. Light interface (design level).** One definition, compiled for both targets. "Absent" is expressed in the type, never as a sentinel value; a distant light reports `is_infinite`, not a distance of 1e30.
  - `LightSampleContext { p, n_geom, n_shade, kind: point | segment(o, d, t_max) }` (no light-linking set, ruling 45). The segment form serves medium NEE (Conty Estevez and Kulla 2018 §5.3; 0006).
  - `sample_li(ctx, u2, λ[4]) → { wi, dist_or_infinite, pdf_solid_angle, is_delta, p_light }`, deterministic in (ctx, u2).
  - `pdf_li(ctx, wi, hit) → pdf`.
  - `sample_le(u2_pos, u2_dir, λ[4], target_sphere) → { ray, Le, pdf_pos, pdf_dir }`, for light paths (pbrt-v4 `Light::SampleLe` covers every type).
  - `power(weighting) → scalar` for D10's importance and radiometric power, and `bounds() → { bbox, θ_o, θ_e, two_sided }` for the tree.
- **D8. Lamp intersection.** One crossing per step, no cap.
  - Area and sphere lamps are primitives in the scene acceleration structure: rectangles as two triangles, spheres as OptiX built-in spheres, disks and ellipses as one custom primitive with a purely geometric intersection program (Embree: user geometry). Lamp instances carry the light's ray-visibility bits and are excluded from shadow-ray visibility, so lamps never block shadow rays (bit allocation: 0002).
  - `route_hit` sends a lamp hit to a small `shade_light` stage. For camera paths it adds the MIS-weighted, front-facing Le (lamp emission programs, IES and node graphs, run by the VM as emission-only programs, 0005), weighted by transmittance in a medium (0006), to the sample's slot (D5). It then re-queues `intersect_closest` from just past the lamp with the same direction; bounce counters and the event index do not change. Light paths pass through lamps with no contribution.
  - Cost: one extra step and one traversal per lamp crossed, only on rays that cross a lamp; crossings are counted (D29). This is Cycles' structure (`kernel/integrator/shade_light.h` re-queuing `intersect_closest`).
- **D9. Emissive geometry.**
  - Every emissive triangle is a light, i.e. a leaf of the light sampler.
  - Power estimate = area × π × emission from the material compiler's constant or texture-average estimate (0005), weighted as in D10.
  - Sampling: spherical triangle (Arvo 1995) when the subtended solid angle is in pbrt-v4 `Triangle::Sample`'s range, otherwise uniform area.
  - A triangle with estimate 0 has selection pmf 0, also under the D10 floor, so its BSDF hits take MIS weight 1. Required for unbiasedness and tested.
  - Sidedness follows Blender as read by 0005: θ_e = π/2 for one-sided emitters and π for two-sided ones.
- **D10. Light selection.**
  - `LightSampler { sample(ctx, u) → (light, pmf); pmf(ctx, light) }`.
  - **Importance weighting.** w(λ) = R̂_film(λ) + R̂_exc(λ): R̂_film is the normalised sum of the active film responses (CMFs for display, band curves for science); R̂_exc is the normalised sum of the excitation spectra of the fluorescent materials in the scene (0005 FLUORESCENT), absent when there are none. A light's importance energy is ∫ S(λ) w(λ) dλ, never luminance. Fluorescence moves transport wavelengths into the excitation band (0003 `rewrite_wavelength`), so a UV lamp lighting a brightener has importance even when only the display film is active.
  - **Floor.** pmf(ℓ) = (1 − ε)·p_imp(ℓ) + ε·p_rad(ℓ), where p_rad is proportional to radiometric power ∫ S(λ) dλ over all wavelengths and ε = 0.05 (PROVISIONAL; defensive mixture, Hesterberg 1995). `sample` draws from a radiometric alias table with probability ε and otherwise from the importance sampler; `pmf` returns the mixture. Every light with non-zero power therefore has a non-zero pmf, which delta lights need because NEE is their only strategy.
  - **Importance sampler.** A light tree, on by default (Conty Estevez and Kulla 2018), following Cycles `kernel/light/tree.h` and `scene/light_tree.cpp`: distance clamp max(0.5·|centroid − bbox.max|, d); minimum/maximum importance blend; distant lights and the background inside the tree; stack-free descent with a bit trail for `pmf`; one tree per scene, since light and shadow linking is not designed in (ruling 45).
  - An alias table over *all* lights, environment and sun included (pbrt-v4 `AliasTable`, `PowerLightSampler`), implements the same interface and is the tree's test oracle. Fixed-ratio environment-versus-lamp splitting is not used (v1 rejected it).
  - **M1b builder.** The tree is stored as 64-byte nodes (box, unit cone axis, θ_o, θ_e and summed importance energy as float, then two child indices or one light index, and leaf and distant flags) plus an 8-byte bit trail and depth per light; the minimal host builder gives each light of positive importance its own leaf (an importance-0 light gets none, so its pmf is 0), splits at the median of box centres on the largest axis of their bounds with ties broken by light index, merges boxes and cones as Cycles `merge` does, puts the infinite lights under the root's right child when local lights exist (otherwise they are the whole tree) and splits them by index where Cycles keeps one distant leaf, takes as node energy each light's on-axis intensity from its D10 importance (ruling 90, task 154; P/(4π) for point and sphere lamps, P/π for area lamps, L·A for triangles with L the average emission, π times the average radiance at distance 1 for the world, following Cycles' builder, `scene/light_tree.cpp` of the v5.3.0 snapshot; the alias table and the floor keep power: Conty Estevez and Kulla's importance Φ cos / d² ranks emitters by irradiance only within one emitter type, and with power as node energy isotropic lamps are weighted 4x against one-sided area lamps and the world's 4π²R²L grows with scene size against local P / d²; the slice-B traversal task measures the D10 tree row's 2x variance target with these energies), treats a cone with θ_o + θ_e = π as emitting everywhere (≥ where Cycles has >), and weighs a leaf by the node importance, while Cycles' SAOH splits, multi-light leaves and per-type emitter importance are M4.
- **D11. Environment importance.**
  - The table is built by evaluating world radiance, weighted by w(λ), on an equal-area square grid (pbrt-v4 `EqualAreaSquareToSphere`, after Clarberg 2008): 1024² by default (PROVISIONAL; Cycles' default world map resolution is 1024), evaluated on the GPU and rebuilt only on world edits.
  - `k_bake_environment` writes cell `iv n + iu` (n an argument, 1024 by default) as the radiance at the cell centre summed over the observer grid with w(λ) in float, running the world program unscaled (no radiance scale s, 0003 d10), and the host divides the grid by its maximum before `build_env_table` (the table is a density, so it does not depend on the world's absolute scale), keeping the maximum and the solid-angle mean of the un-normalised grid in the authored world's display units; the bake runs before the light table is built, at most once per world edit, keyed on the world program, its constant block and the images it samples (task 225).
  - **Light type and importance** (task 173; write-back, ruling 87). A program world whose bake has a table is the light `WORLD_ENV`; a constant world stays `WORLD_CONST`; a black program world (no table) keeps `WORLD_CONST` with importance 0, so nothing samples it and an escaped ray takes MIS weight 1; a refused grid leaves no world light (misses render black). `WORLD_ENV`'s importance energy is 4π times the bake's mean (its w(λ)-weighted radiance integrated over the sphere, D10's display weighting) times the record's strength, which carries s as every other light's display energy does (lead ruling 2026-11-25, 225's split); its power factor is πR², so the importance is 4π² R² mean · s (pbrt-v4 `ImageInfiniteLight::Phi`).
  - Sampled with pbrt-v4 `PiecewiseConstant2D`. NEE uses the MIS-compensated distribution (Karlík et al. 2019; pbrt-v4 `allowIncompletePDF`), pdf over solid angle the table's pdf / 4π, and `pdf_li` gives that same compensated pdf, so an escaped ray after a non-delta bounce is weighted with pmf · compensated pdf and both strategies of the balance heuristic read one density; `pdf_li(sample.wi) == sample.pdf` holds under ruling 88's rule (Verification D7/D11). An estimator without BSDF sampling cannot use the compensated pdf, which is 0 where the radiance is below its mean: the `light_only` test mode (D1 row) samples the plain table. Light-path emission (`sample_le`) uses the uncompensated one and is M4; until then `WORLD_ENV`'s `sample_le` is refused.
  - Radiance at a sampled direction always comes from the world program, never from the table. NEE writes the shadow item with everything but Le (the MIS weight and the record's strength included), sets `item_flags` bit `ITEM_WORLD_LE` and the item's `lambda_t` (0003 d20), and the shadow stage runs the world program along the item's direction on delivery (no ray type, depth 0, as the bake): the world's interpreter stays out of `shade_surface` (0002 D10 ladder; B-05, owner decision 40).
- **D12. Sky and sun.** This record owns the sky model (0005's `SKY` op evaluates it).
  - **Model.** Blender's Single Scattering (Nishita et al. 1993) and Multiple Scattering sky types, enumerated from RNA at implementation. Ported from Cycles `intern/sky/source/sky_single_scattering.cpp` (Apache-2.0) and `sky_multiple_scattering.cpp` (MIT), with shader-side evaluation and the sun-disc limb profile from `kernel/svm/sky.h` (Apache-2.0). `sky_nishita.h` and `sky_math.h` (GPL-2.0-or-later) are neither read nor copied (`v1-lessons/blender-integration.md`, `research/lights.md`). Preetham and Hosek-Wilkie are not designed here; selecting them shows a visible error.
  - **Spectral evaluation.** On each world edit the host precomputes, in double, a sky radiance table over view direction relative to the sun × the source's own wavelength samples (table size PROVISIONAL), keeping the source's absolute units; the `SKY` op interpolates it at the transport wavelengths and clamps outside its range with a report entry (0003, ranges). If a source turns out to compute only XYZ or RGB, that type is upsampled as an illuminant (0003) and reported `approximated`. No fitted exposure constant (v1); Blender's strength and sun-intensity inputs multiply the result, under 0003 decision 11's unit anchor (ruling 35).
  - **Sun-disc split.** When the disc is enabled and the compiler's sky-split output (0005 item 43) proves that the world output radiance is G·Sky(ω) + R(ω), with G a direction-independent spectrum after constant folding (the chain from the `SKY` op to the output contains only ops linear in that input with constant factors: Background strength, Multiply, Add, Mix with a constant factor), the disc becomes a distant-light leaf: irradiance G·E_disc independent of disc size, the source's limb profile applied identically in NEE and in BSDF-hit evaluation, camera-visible (v1). The `SKY` op then evaluates without the disc, and the importance table excludes it, so sky and sun are two lights with separate pdfs (v1 measured a 1.3% low bias from a profile used only in NEE).
  - Otherwise the disc stays in the world program and is sampled only through the table, and a visible warning names the node that broke linearity, which the same compiler output reports (the fail-loudly rule of CLAUDE.md).

### C. Sampler use

- **D13. Sampler.** The sampler, its dimension map, its light-path keys and its independent mode are 0003's (ruling 3). This record adds no sampler, no blue-noise mode and no reserved dimension.
- **D14. What transport requires of 0003's dimension map.**
  - The per-bounce block index is the path's **event index**, stored in 0003's `event_index` field (ruling 26): it advances at every vertex that makes a random decision, including opacity pass-throughs, which do not advance Blender's bounce counters (D3). Without this, stacked alpha cards reuse one uniform: 10 layers at α = 0.5 would pass together with probability 0.5 instead of 0.5^10. Lamp crossings make no decision and do not advance it.
  - Per event, transport consumes uniforms for: lobe or event choice (Mix Shader selection then lobe selection share one uniform by rescaling, pbrt-v4 `SampleDiscrete`; also the opacity decision); a 2D direction; light pick (the D10 floor branch rescales it); a 2D light position; Russian roulette; the extension draw (grating order, fluorescence λi).
- **D15. Light paths** draw every random number (light pick, position, direction, wavelengths, time, lobe, extension, Russian roulette, the connection's lens sample) from 0003's light-path keys.

### D. Adaptive sampling

- **D16. Stop metric and schedule.** The film keeps per pixel the full sum and the class-A sum S1A (0008), so per channel c (X, Y, Z for display, or each sensor band) the mean I_c and the class-A estimate A_c = 2·S1A_c/n exist.
  - Error per channel: e_c = |I_c − A_c| / (ε + g(I_c)), g(x) = √x for x < 1 and x otherwise, ε = 1e-4. This is Cycles' `film/adaptive_sampling.h` form (SOURCE), applied per channel instead of summed over channels. Cycles scales by film exposure; here the inputs are pre-exposure values, because `film_exposure` is display-only and converged flags must not go stale. At exposure 1 the threshold means what it means in Cycles. Science bands are scaled by the band's image mean (PROVISIONAL).
  - A pixel has converged when max_c e_c < threshold. The summed form is rejected because a dim channel adds little to the numerator and nothing to the normaliser (research 04 §7; v1 blue floor; Dammertz et al. 2010 use colour noise).
  - Schedule (Cycles, SOURCE): check when (n + 1) mod 16 = 0, after min samples (Blender setting, 0 = Cycles' auto formula); dilate the unconverged mask by one pixel in x and then in y; `film_build_active` leaves converged pixels out of the active-pixel list, which `generate_camera` reads.
  - **Tail rule.** When fewer than half the pixels are active, a pass gives each of the u active pixels k = min(⌊N_pix/u⌋, samples left until the next check) samples, so launches stay full, a pass never holds more than N_pix samples (the per-sample buffer's capacity, 0008), and no pass crosses a check.
  - With light tracing on, A and B include the light image split by pass parity (0008).
  - Adaptive sampling is off in DLSS mode and in every variance or firefly measurement.

### E. Firefly and validity policy

- **D17. No biased estimator is applied by default.** No clamp, no Filter Glossy, no path regularisation (Kaplanyan and Dachsbacher 2013), no firefly reweighting (Zirr, Hanika, Dachsbacher 2018), no outlier rejection.
  - Allowed variance reduction is only unbiased: NEE, MIS, Russian roulette, light tree, environment importance, the light-tracing partition (D18) and adaptive stopping.
  - Biased controls exist only as explicit user settings that default to off: direct and indirect clamp, applied per contribution before it is added (D5), on the maximum film channel with chroma preserved ("direct" = emission found at bounce index − 1 ≤ 0, per v1's Cycles finding); Filter Glossy, a roughness floor applied at exactly one point in the material stage (0005); the reflective and refractive caustics toggles, which also disable the light-tracing pass.
  - Every active biased control is listed in 0001's per-render report and in output metadata. A render that asks for science outputs (0008) while a biased control is active fails with a visible error.
  - A non-finite sample is not written, is counted (`INVALID`, 0003's termination enum; 0008's invalid counter) and reported. Any test tier fails on a non-zero count.

### F. Light tracing for gratings, prisms and caustics

- **D18. Method: light tracing with camera connections, restricted to caustic paths.** Particle tracing with direct computation of pixel values: Arvo 1986; Dutré, Lafortune, Willems 1993; Veach 1997 ch. 10; reference implementations pbrt-v4 `LightPathIntegrator` and Mitsuba 3 `ptracer.cpp`. Path space is partitioned as in Jensen's caustic photon map (Jensen 1996, 2001). Unbiased; handles medium scattering vertices (a beam in fog after a grating); no density-estimation grid.
- **D19. Exact partition rules.**
  - **One delta predicate.** `lobe_is_delta`, one function in 0005's lobe library: true for a smooth grating order, smooth dispersive transmission, and any microfacet lobe whose effective roughness after every remap (Filter Glossy included) satisfies pbrt-v4 `TrowbridgeReitzDistribution::EffectivelySmooth` (α < 1e-3, SOURCE; 0005's `MAY_BE_DELTA` uses the same threshold). Camera sampling, light-path sampling and the compiler all call it.
  - **Casters.** An instance is a caster when its material has `MAY_BE_DELTA` (0005, conservative) and its `READS_RAY_TYPE` flag is clear (0005 item 43: no Light Path ray-type or depth output reachable). Ray-type-dependent materials are not reciprocal, so no partition through them is exact: their delta interactions are path-traced only and the report lists them. This keeps Blender's "glass transparent to shadow rays" trick behaving as it does in Cycles, with no double counting from light paths. 0007 exposes a per-object opt-out (default automatic).
  - **Ownership.** Each vertex is labelled by the lobe that scattered there. The light pass owns exactly the labelled paths L δ+ X E in which the first δ vertex (from the light) is on a caster, X is a non-delta-labelled surface or medium vertex, X is not a vertex that rewrites wavelengths (a FLUORESCENT surface, 0005 item 26, or an inelastic medium event, 0006 D15; ruling 44), X is seen directly by the camera, and no segment is curved. The path tracer owns every other path.
  - **Light-path side.**
    - At x₁ (the first vertex after emission) the light path must hit a caster and sample a delta lobe, otherwise it is discarded. It never connects at x₁, because L X E is direct light, which the path tracer owns.
    - At every later vertex reached through delta samples only: at a vertex that rewrites wavelengths the light path ends without connecting; otherwise, if f there has a non-delta part (surface lobes or a medium phase function), connect to the camera with that part only. Then continue if that vertex samples a delta lobe, otherwise terminate. A mixed vertex (a roughness-0 coat over a diffuse base) therefore connects and may also continue, and the next non-delta vertex connects again.
    - Delta vertices after x₁ may be on any object. A light path that enters the curved region terminates (`LIGHT_PATH_IN_REGION`, 0003).
  - **Camera side.** The path is armed at x₁ (its first vertex) when x₁ sampled a non-delta lobe or phase and is not a vertex that rewrites wavelengths. Each later delta sample sets `caster_bit = is_caster(object)`; a non-delta sample or a curved segment (0006) disarms, and the path never re-arms. Emission found while armed, after at least one delta vertex, with `caster_bit` set, is dropped (lamp crossing, emissive surface, environment or sun); the path continues. `caster_bit` holds the caster flag of the delta vertex nearest the light, which is the light path's x₁. NEE is unaffected because casters block shadow rays (D4).
  - **Segment symmetry.** Every rule the camera path applies to a segment, the light path applies to the same segment:
    - lamp visibility: discard at x₁ if the light is invisible to the ray type of the lobe sampled there (glossy for reflection, transmission for refraction);
    - object visibility: segments after a delta reflection or transmission trace with the glossy or transmission mask; no connection from an X that is invisible to camera rays; at X, the diffuse and glossy parts of f (the split 0008 already asks of 0005) are zeroed when the delta object on the next segment is invisible to that part's ray type;
    - holdout at X scales the connection by (1 − holdout weight); shadow-catcher receivers get no connections, and the report says so when casters exist;
    - media: free flight uses the same transmittance estimator; a real scatter on a delta chain is a non-delta vertex (connect, terminate).
  - State: 3 path-flag bits, armed, chain non-empty and `caster_bit` (0003's flag table).
- **D20. Light-path emission.** A pass's light work is P light paths (0002's work counter).
  - Light choice: an alias table over every light type (point, spot, sphere, area, sun, environment, emissive triangles) by flux toward the casters' bounding sphere, weighted with w(λ) and given the same ε radiometric floor as D10. Every light with non-zero power there gets a non-zero pmf; otherwise the camera-side cull would remove caustics with no replacement.
  - Direction: uniform in the cone subtending the casters' bounding sphere; for distant and environment lights, a position on the disc that covers the sphere (v1). This loses nothing because x₁ must be a caster.
  - Wavelengths: film wavelengths and their pdf come from the same sensor wavelength table as camera paths and are recomputed from the light path's sampler key wherever needed (0 bytes of state, ruling 7); transport wavelengths start equal to them. Sampling proportional to an emitter's spectrum is not designed in; narrow-line emitters are a known noise case to measure (Risks).
  - Weights follow `sample_le`: beta₀ = Le(λt)·|cos| / (P·pmf·pdf_pos·pdf_dir) per lane.
  - P: a controller keeps the light pass at or under 0009 B-36's share of pass time (set by the window sweep in Verification), clamped to [one full wave, the main-pool capacity], with each launch inside 0009 B-17 (ruling 14). One full wave is DERIVED as 70 SMs × 2 blocks × 256 threads = 35,840 (spike A §1 and 0002); the ceiling is 0002's pool capacity. In final renders P equals the camera samples per pass (PROVISIONAL).
- **D21. Kernels and state.**
  - 0002's `generate_light` and `shade_light_path` (`ShadeKind::Light`), plus a light variant of each extension identity that 0005 defines; extension hits on light paths route there, so `shade_light_path` contains no extension code (spine §2; 0002).
  - Light-path vertices call the same material, VM and lobe functions as camera paths in *importance* mode: no 1/η² radiance scaling, and the Veach 1997 ch. 5 shading-normal correction. No NEE.
  - Light paths are main-pool items marked by 0002's `path_kind` bit, with the same path state as camera paths, including the 20 B medium stack when volumes exist (0006, ruling 8). They share `intersect_closest`, `shade_light` (pass-through), the medium stage (0006 emits a connection item, not an NEE item, at a light-path elastic scatter vertex; at an inelastic one the light path ends, D19) and the shadow stage. A connection item carries a film position, the light-group id, the light path's sampler key and its packet flags in place of a sample slot.
- **D22. Wavelengths on light paths.** At a wavelength-dependent delta vertex (dispersion, grating order) the path calls `terminate_secondaries` (0003) and lane 0, the lane it keeps, continues (0003 decision 5; ruling 24); the light path's random hero lane (ruling 4) serves only hero-sampled decisions before that. Lane-0 wavelengths differ between light paths, so one pass already fills a continuous spectrum. A receiver is evaluated at the light path's own transport wavelengths, and a splat converts to film channels with `film_weights` at the film wavelengths and pdf recomputed from the light path's key (0003), so nothing mismatches.
- **D23. Connection and splat.**
  - The connection computes beta·f·|cos|·We/pdf through the camera's `sample_wi` (pbrt-v4 `PerspectiveCamera::We`, `PDF_We`, `SampleWi`) with a lens sample and the light path's time. Every camera type 0007 decision 36 covers implements `We`, `PDF_We` and `sample_wi` (owner decision 25): perspective and thin lens as pbrt-v4 does; orthographic, whose direction is fixed per film point, by projecting the vertex onto the film along the view direction (through the lens sample when depth of field is on); the panoramic types through the inverse of their ray mapping and its Jacobian (We as defined in Veach 1997 ch. 10).
  - **Perspective entry (task 053, R63).** The camera is a pinhole: rays start at the camera origin (scene origin folded in, 0003 d26) through the film point that `projection_inverse` (Blender's NDC to camera space, 4x4 row-major, applied to (ndc_x, ndc_y, 0, 1) and divided by w) gives, rotated by `cam_to_world`. The film maps affinely onto the plane z = -1 of camera space (the camera looks down -z), also under Blender's shift; the host stores that plane's corner and extent (a skewed projection is reported). Film area A is the plane area of the whole film. For a ray at angle θ from the view axis inside the frustum: `We = 1 / (A cos⁴θ)`, `PDF_We = (pos 1, dir 1 / (A cos³θ))` (pos 1: the lens is a point), 0 outside. `sample_wi(ref)` takes wi = unit(origin − ref), the film pixel of the ray along −wi, `We` as above and pdf = d² / cos θ (the lens-point pdf 1 converted to solid angle at ref; pbrt-v4 `PerspectiveCamera::SampleWi`, lens area 1). Film pixels are x right, y up from the bottom row (Blender's buffer order). Ref: pbrt-v4 `src/pbrt/cameras.cpp` `PerspectiveCamera::We`, `PDF_We`, `SampleWi`; second sources pbrt-v3 §16.1.1 and Veach 1997 ch. 10. `cam_to_world` has its scale removed as Cycles' Blender camera sync does, and a remaining shear raises `CAMERA_UNSUPPORTED` (detail `SHEAR`). Orthographic and panoramic cameras, and depth of field, raise `CAMERA_UNSUPPORTED` (0001 D2.11) and render as this pinhole until M4/M5.
  - A connection passes 0006 D28's gate near the curved region; camera paths whose segment is curved are disarmed (D19), so caustics gated out here stay with them.
  - The shadow stage delivers an unoccluded connection to 0008's `SPLAT` plane over the same normalised pixel-filter footprint as camera samples (0008; ruling 2). The value already includes 1/P.
  - Displayed result = path-traced mean + light-image mean (0008). Light passes continue while any pixel is unconverged. The light image is not bit-reproducible; reproducibility tests exclude it.
- **D24. Coverage limits, loud.**
  - Every camera type gets light-pass connections (D23), so directly seen caustics from point lamps and spotlights render whatever the camera.
  - Two path families are path-traced only, so they are **missing for delta lights** (point, spot, zero-angle sun, collimated area): caustics seen through a delta chain (L δ+ D δ+ E: a pool floor through water, a caustic in a mirror) and caustic light that bounces again (L δ+ D D+ E). For other lights they are path-traced, unbiased but slow. Point lamps and spotlights do cast caustics onto directly seen surfaces through the light pass; these two families may be missing in 2.0 with a visible warning (owner decision 25), so the per-render report names both as a warning whenever casters and delta lights are present.
  - Direct light through smooth glass (windows) is carried only by the light pass: NEE is blocked (D4) and BSDF hits on the light are culled (D19). Window-lit interiors therefore depend on the light-pass share (D20) and are a benchmark scene (Verification). Their second bounce is the path-traced L δ+ D D E family above.

### G. Progressive accumulation and reset

- **D25. Passes and reset rules.**
  - A pass is one sample for every active pixel (k samples in the adaptive tail, D16) plus one light pass of P light paths if casters exist. The sample index per pixel is monotonic within an epoch, so the Sobol prefix holds.
  - 0007 classifies edits, owns render control and defines generation (a version of scene state) and epoch (the accumulation reset counter) (0007 decision 25; ruling 37). When a new generation starts a new epoch (an image-affecting edit), film, light image, adaptive state and statistics are cleared and sample indices restart at 0; the light tree and environment table are rebuilt only if lights, emissive materials or the world changed. Display-only edits start no epoch.
  - Writes carry the epoch and a stale epoch is discarded. Cancellation latency is one step (0002).
  - Reset latency and light-structure build time are 0009 B-22 and B-23; light-structure build counts toward 0007's first-frame gate.
  - DLSS mode (0008): the colour film resets every frame (statistics do not); 1 spp per frame from 0003's independent sampler mode, one light pass per frame, adaptive off. The viewport sample limit and final renders use the same loop.

### H. Later features and film inputs

- **D26. No hooks for later reuse or guiding.** ReSTIR-class reuse (Bitterli et al. 2020; Lin et al. 2022) and path guiding (Müller, Gross, Novák 2017) are not designed here. No state, dimension, runtime flag or branch is reserved for them (the scope rule of CLAUDE.md; G4). What they would need and already exists for other reasons: light samples deterministic in (ctx, u2) (D7), the D10 importance as a target function, `pdf` on every lobe, and 0008's guide buffers.
- **D27. Film inputs owed to 0008.**
  - Light groups: every contribution carries the u8 group id of its emitter (lamp, emissive mesh, world): shadow items the id of the sampled light, emission hits and lamp crossings the id of the emitter hit, connections the id of the emitting light. Enabled group planes receive it beside the sample slot.
  - Light-path passes: when enabled, each contribution carries its category, fixed at the first scattering vertex and split direct/indirect by bounce count (0008). The per-category weights are 0008's conditional state.
  - Denoising surface: the path sets 0008's denoising-surface flag bit at its first non-delta-lobe sample; the surface stage writes 0008's guides there.

### I. Statistics and diagnostics

- **D28. Per-sample statistics.** The path-end stage reports to 0008's statistics channels: bounce count (0008's definition), termination reason and, when the time map is on, per-path stage cycles (0003's conditional `stats_time`). Termination reasons are 0003's enum; per-type limits map to `MAX_BOUNCE`, a sample with f = 0 or pdf = 0 to `ZERO_THROUGHPUT`. Storage, defaults and widths are 0008's. Nothing saturates.
- **D29. Global counters per pass (u64):** items per stage and per bounce; active paths; light paths emitted, connected and discarded at x₁ (by reason: not a caster, non-delta at x₁, visibility); culled camera contributions; lamp crossings; shadow rays and transparent-layer re-queues; per-kernel GPU time (events). They feed the benchmark HTML report and 0001's per-render report. The heat-map display belongs to 0007 and 0008.
- **Kernels and programs this record needs in 0002's list:** `generate_light`, `shade_light_path` and a light variant per extension identity; `shade_light` with `route_hit` routing for lamp hits; lamp primitives (OptiX built-in spheres; one custom geometric intersection program for disks and ellipses); the environment importance table build (D11). 0008 supplies the per-sample buffer resolve.

- **Accumulation.** Per-contribution atomics straight into film moments give no per-sample second moment (0008). A per-path accumulator in the path state loses or misroutes shadow results when a path ends or its slot is refilled first; a `shadow_pending` gate would keep waiting paths in the pool and still race when transparent shadows keep two items of one path in flight. The per-sample buffer (ruling 1, 0008) has neither problem.
- **Power heuristic vs the pbrt volpath balance form.** The power heuristic can be slightly lower in variance, but a second form beside the r_u/r_l form that chromatic media need duplicates code. One formulation wins (ruling 5).
- **Capped lamp hits per segment** (v1's 4): silently drops crossings and needs repeated traversals without any-hit programs. D8's one crossing per step has no cap.
- **Importance over the film responses only** starves UV and other non-visible emitters whose light reaches the film through fluorescence; with no floor, such a delta light is never sampled.
- **One splat pixel chosen by filter weight:** same expectation as footprint splatting and one atomic instead of up to four, but rejected by ruling 2 so the light image uses exactly the camera film's filter treatment.
- **Light-path wavelengths drawn from the emitter's spectrum:** would help narrow-line emitters but needs stored wavelengths and pdf per light path; rejected by ruling 7.
- **Separate environment strategy at a fixed ratio:** v1 measured higher variance with lamp plus HDRI, and a tree that carried only some light types starved the sun. Every light type goes into one selection.
- **Blue-noise sample ordering** (Cycles `kernel/sample/pattern.h`, after Ahmed and Wonka 2020: Morton-ordered Owen scrambling). Some of its modes need the sample count up front, and its benefit under OIDN in the viewport is unmeasured. The sampler is 0003's; adopting it would be a 0003 change backed by research 04 S6.
- **Adaptive metrics:** luminance-only (v1 blue floor) and Cycles' summed form (colour-blind by construction) are both rejected.
- **Default clamping (Cycles' practice):** it caps real focused energy (v1 capped a caustic focus) and contradicts "physically correct always".
- **Reserved hooks for ReSTIR and guiding:** rejected under the scope rule of CLAUDE.md and G4 (D26).

## Consequences

- **Easy:** new light types, since each implements one interface and the tree, NEE, MIS and light-path emission follow; new extension lobes appear in light paths through their light variant; light-pass correctness is checked against path tracing with area lights by the partition tests; reset and resume bugs show as bit differences on the single-threaded CPU.
- **Hard:** the D24 gaps for delta lights; scenes with very large casters waste light paths (most x₁ hits sample a non-delta lobe), so the per-object opt-out matters; window-lit interiors lean on the light pass; the light image is non-deterministic and outside per-pixel sampling control; every material must declare `MAY_BE_DELTA` correctly, or caustics vanish.
- **Cost when unused:** no casters: no light-path launches, the cull bits never set, one check per pass; no lamps crossed: no `shade_light` launches; no fluorescent materials: R̂_exc absent; adaptive off: no class-A sum; ReSTIR and guiding: zero. The per-sample buffer is always allocated (0007's budget table).

## What must exist from the start, and what can wait

Milestones are assigned only in `docs/ROADMAP.md` (G1).

**Must exist from the first milestone, because retrofitting is expensive:**
- No radiance in the path state; every contribution to the sample slot (D5), and the sample slot in every shadow item. Reason: changing it later changes the path state and shadow item layouts and every shade kernel's write path.
- beta, r_u, r_l as 4-lane spectra with the balance form (D1). Reason: path state layout and every MIS weight in every kernel.
- Light paths as main-pool items with `path_kind`, the connection item (film position, sampler key, packet flags) and the 3 partition flag bits (D19, D21). Reason: shadow item layout, path state flags, and the shadow stage's signature.
- The light interface with `sample_le` and the segment context, and camera `We`/`PDF_We`/`sample_wi` (D7, D23). Reason: every light type and the camera implement these signatures; adding them later rewrites each.
- Event-index semantics of the dimension map (D14). Reason: changing it later changes every random sequence and invalidates every pinned reference result.
- Lamp primitives and `shade_light` routing (D8). Reason: lamp crossings define the event index, the emitter-hit MIS and the kernel list.
- Light-group id in shadow and connection items (D27), epoch tags on writes (D25), the termination enum and statistics counters (D28-D29; spine §3). Reason: item layouts and kernel signatures.
- `prev_ng`, `prev_ns`, `prev_dist` in the path state (D1). Reason: emitter-hit MIS must see the same context as NEE; adding them later changes the path state.

**Can be added later without redesign:** the light tree (behind the alias table's interface, D10); the radiometric floor and excitation weighting (D10); the sky model and sun-disc split (D12); IES, spot blend, area spread, ellipses; per-type bounce limits beyond total; colour-aware adaptive sampling with the tail rule (D16); the explicit biased controls (D17); the light pass itself (D18-D24) on the interfaces above; light extension variants; per-object caster opt-out; light-group and light-path planes (D27); a fix for the D24 gaps (probabilistic PPM on D21's light paths or MNEE, chosen by measurement); Preetham and Hosek-Wilkie skies; ReSTIR and path guiding, each only with a measured gain; Wilkie hero MIS for rough dispersive lobes (0003).

## Risks and open items

- **Camera importance for orthographic and panoramic cameras** (D23) has no reference implementation (pbrt-v4 implements perspective only); the D23 check covers each type.
- **Narrow-line emitters** on light paths (ruling 7): measure the noise of a sodium line through a prism against an emitter-spectrum proposal.
- **D24 gaps for delta lights** (seen-through and indirect caustics): the most visible gap in pools and mirrors. Probabilistic PPM needs a wavelength-matching rule; MNEE needs per-wavelength constraints. Measure both. The owner accepts the gap in 2.0 with a visible warning (owner decision 25).
- **Window-lit interiors:** direct sun through glass is light-pass only; if no fixed share is within 2x of the best share on both the caustic scenes and the window scene, a share driven by the light image's fraction of measured variance is the candidate.
- **Light-path efficiency:** one cone toward the union of casters wastes light paths when casters are spread out or large. Measure light paths connected per light path emitted on the canonical scenes; per-caster-cluster cones are the remedy.
- **Early viewport look:** unbiased splats sparkle at first. Check by eye with OIDN at 1-16 passes.
- **Texture filtering on light paths:** with no ray cone, the receiver texture is filtered differently from path tracing. Use the camera-projected footprint at the connection vertex; measure the difference on a textured receiver.
- **Light-tree build time** for 1M emissive triangles against 0007's first-frame gate; GPU or LBVH-style builds if over budget.
- **Importance weighting** with sun plus mesh emitters, infrared sources and fluorescent scenes (research 04 S8): measure selection rates and tune ε.
- **Per-sample buffer cost:** fp32 atomics per contribution and one resolve per pass; measured with 0008.
- **Lamp crossings:** many large visible lamps add steps; measure on the 64-lamp scene against 0002's step budget.
- **Sky sources:** whether the Multiple Scattering source is spectral, and its table size, are confirmed when it is read.
- **Spectral MIS after a wavelength rewrite** (fluorescence, redshift) is 0003 decision 7's. The grating efficiency formulas are cited but not yet re-read (0005 item 24); the grating test waits on that.
