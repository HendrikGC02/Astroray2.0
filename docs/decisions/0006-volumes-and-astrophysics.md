# 0006 — Volumes, participating media and the astrophysics core

Status: proposed (2026-11-14), revised after review (2026-11-14), reconciled with `RULINGS.md` (2026-11-14)

## Context

Owner decision 7 puts volumes in the first usable release, in full breadth: homogeneous and grid media, world volume, Blender's volume shaders, emission and blackbody. Owner decision 8 makes the first astrophysics target a black hole inside an emission nebula. Decision 19 asks fluorescence to cover astrophysical cases too. Decision 17 brings the light-tracing pass, which must cross media (0004 D18-D23). Milestones are `docs/ROADMAP.md`'s business (G1). This record owns the medium interface, the grid format and the spacetime implementation (RULINGS, Ownership): the volume algorithms, the medium stack and nested dielectrics, the Blender volume mapping, grid ingestion, and the astrophysics core: spacetime, geodesic stepping, redshift, the nebula model, line data, science outputs, validation and precision.

Binding constraints. The spine's integrator core includes null-scattering volumes with spectral majorants, homogeneous and grid (§4), amended by C1 and C3 below (ruling 23). Volume/medium work and geodesic stepping each get their own kernel and queue, with records passed by reference (§2); kernel names, queues and resource gates are 0002's, time budgets 0009's. Each path carries 4 rewritable transport wavelengths separate from its film wavelengths, in physical units (§3; 0003). Curved rays advance in segments tested against the ordinary acceleration structure under a compile-time spacetime policy (§3; 0003 D28 owns the policy interface); `advance_geodesic` is a CUDA kernel and no material, medium or VM code runs inside OptiX programs (ruling 18). The target needs emission and absorption along curved rays and no scattering integrator. The CPU reference is double; float on the GPU only if its measured error near the photon ring is acceptable (§7). Node graphs are evaluated by the VM (§5, 0005). The path tracer uses a random hero lane per path (0003 D6, ruling 4) and the balance heuristic in r_u/r_l form (0004 D1, ruling 5) everywhere, media included. The cite-the-source and fail-loudly rules of CLAUDE.md apply. Data policy 0001 D8: tables we generate may be committed; NIST ASD, CHIANTI, ExoMol and Cloudy outputs never are. 0001 D9 admits NanoVDB only through this record.

Evidence. v1 shipped CPU volumes with null scattering, majorant grids and segment NEE, and a CPU-only Kerr tracer validated against GYOTO (`v1-lessons/research/volumes.md`, `gr-astro.md`). Its failures: a world-fog parameter with no consumer rendered vacuum; a volume decision in a saturated shade kernel cost 3.3% on the whole fleet; rays out of steps were silently called "captured"; continuation rays respawned from the entry point of the influence sphere; dense grid transfer is 512 MB at 512³ and "not a long-term format". No spike has measured volumes or geodesics on this machine. Spike A covers surface shading only.

## Decision

### Spine amendments (accepted, ruling 23)

- **C1. Non-scattering media use a deterministic emission-absorption marcher, not null scattering.** For σ_s ≡ 0 (the nebula; fire without smoke; Volume Absorption + Emission) radiance along a segment is a deterministic integral; the GR transfer codes integrate it per sub-step with 2nd-order convergence (ipole `ipolarray.c`; Blacklight eq. 26; KPolaris §2.3, §4.1). Null-collision emission needs v1's artificial emission-majorant floor and adds noise to a noise-free quantity. The geodesic stage needs the marcher anyway, and one estimator inside and outside the curved region avoids a noise seam. Spine §4 reads: "null-scattering volumes with spectral majorants for scattering media; media without scattering use the shared emission-absorption marcher."
- **C2 (clarification).** "No scattering integrator is needed" holds because dust ships as extinction (D32). Scattering inside the curved region is treated as extinction and reported (D29).
- **C3. In volume sockets, Mix Shader and Add Shader combine coefficients linearly.** For volumes the VM output is coefficient-level (D2), and coefficients add physically: σ_t = Σσ_t,i, j = Σj_i (Rybicki and Lightman 1979 §1.4; Cycles sums volume closures, `v1-lessons/research/volumes.md`). A linear sum is exact and costs a few adds. Stochastic Add with weight k produces σ̂ = kσ_i, which can exceed the majorant and break null-collision tracking. Spine §5 reads: "Mix Shader selects stochastically on surfaces; in volume sockets Mix and Add sum coefficients." 0005's Add Shader policy governs surfaces.

### A. Medium interface and data

1. **One lowering path.** Every medium is a VM `volume` program (0005 item 32) that fills a coefficient-level `MediumSample`. The mapping from Blender or Astroray parameters to coefficients lives in exactly one VM op per node kind (D21) or model (D32). `MediumDesc` holds no material parameters. A constant colour is folded by the 0005 compiler to its Jakob-Hanika coefficients (0003 D16), so per-evaluation cost is a sigmoid per lane; folding never removes the program.
   **Layout and mapping (designed in task 179, ruling 87).** A medium's constant block, `MediumBlock` (`src/kernel/medium/medium_block.h`), is 112 B: `SpectrumRef sigma_a`, `SpectrumRef sigma_s[2]`, `SpectrumRef emission`, `f32 g[2]`, `u32 lobe_count`, `u32 entry`. Every coefficient is in m⁻¹ with its density already folded into the ref's scale (no table lookup per evaluation). Scattering is up to two lobes: `sigma_s[k]` is lobe k's phase weight and its own ref, since a sum of two sigmoid spectra is not one ref, and `g[k]` is its Henyey-Greenstein anisotropy, stored within ±`kHgMaxAbsG`. `emission` holds a CONSTANT 0 until a medium emits. The Blender mapping is one function, `volume_term_ref` (`vm/ops/volume_coeffs.h`), from Cycles `svm_node_closure_volume`: a Volume Scatter term is colour × density, a Volume Absorption term is (1 − colour) × density, density clamped to ≥ 0. The colour is upsampled as a reflectance first (D21). The absorber's complement is the same RGB ref with its three sigmoid coefficients negated, because 1 − S(x) = S(−x) for the Jakob-Hanika sigmoid: one ref, exact at a grey 0 or 1. Scene units are metres in material constants (0005 item 2), so a density arrives in m⁻¹ and no metres-per-unit factor is applied until the scene table exists. The compiler (`compile_volume`) folds a VOLUME graph whose inputs are constant to one block: one absorption term and up to two scatter lobes; colour and density inputs that are not constant, an op with no volume meaning (`ops.def` `volume_reachable` false), a non-finite value, a second absorber and a third lobe are refused and reported (PROGRAM_REJECTED naming the node, or NODE_UNSUPPORTED) and the material has no medium while its surface is unaffected. A clamped colour, density or anisotropy is reported once each as NODE_APPROXIMATED.
2. **`medium_eval(const MediumDesc&, const Point&, const Lambda& lambda_rest, MediumSample&)`** runs the program and returns, at the path's 4 wavelengths in the medium's rest frame:
   - `Spec sigma_a, sigma_s` in m⁻¹;
   - `Spec emission`: the emission coefficient j_λ in W m⁻³ sr⁻¹ nm⁻¹ (radiance added per metre; not σ_a·L_e, since Blender's emission does not scale with density and astrophysical emissivities are given as j);
   - `PhaseLobe phase[2]`, each `{u8 kind, f32 p0, f32 p1, Spec weight}` with weight = that component's σ_s. Two lobes cover a Mix or Add of two scattering volumes; a third distinct lobe is reported unsupported;
   - `Vec3 velocity` in m/s relative to the static frame, read only by NEBULAR (stage-local temporary, free when unread).
   Media are static in 2.0: no time argument.
   **Entry and signature as built (task 179, ruling 87).** The volume entry is the same interpreter in a second mode, `run_volume_entry`: the medium's block in, the `MediumSample` out, `ShadeContext` carrying only P and the wavelengths. Its one sink is the op `VOLUME_COEFFS` (opcode 18, volume reachable, reads no register): it resolves `sigma_a`, each lobe's `sigma_s` and `emission` from the block with `resolve()` at the four lanes, sets `phase[k] = {HG, g[k], 0, weight = sigma_s[k]}`, `sigma_s` to their sum and velocity to 0. A surface run, a lobe count above two or a ref kind other than CONSTANT or RGB stops with a `VmStatus` instead of reading zeros. Over any box its coefficients are the block's own, so `interval_volume_coeffs` returns `medium_block_sigma_t_bound` (extinction over every wavelength from the refs; a sigmoid ref is scale × [0, 1]) exactly. The function is `medium_eval(const MediumTables&, const MediumDesc&, Real3 p, const Lambda& lambda_rest, VmRegs*, MediumSample*) -> VmStatus` in `src/kernel/medium/medium_eval.h`; `MediumTables` holds the medium program words (word 0 reserved so entry 0 means none), the blocks and the observer. `MediumDesc::program` indexes the blocks, and a block's `entry` is the word offset of its volume entry; the material program's entry-table volume word stays 0. The host keeps blocks and words in `MaterialArrays::media` and `medium_programs`; the device upload and the scene table are tasks 181 and 182.
3. **`MediumDesc`** (scene table, one per medium, 40 B, `static_assert`ed): `u8 model {BLENDER_VOLUME, NEBULAR}`, `u8 estimator` (D9, derived at build: `MARCH_EMISSION_ABSORPTION = 0`, `DELTA_TRACK = 1`), `u16 flags {has_emission, has_scattering, has_velocity, has_fluorescence}` (bits 0-3, named `MEDIUM_HAS_EMISSION`, `MEDIUM_HAS_SCATTERING`, `MEDIUM_HAS_VELOCITY`, `MEDIUM_HAS_FLUORESCENCE` in `src/kernel/medium/medium.h`), `u32 program`, `u32 majorant_grid`, `u32 nebular_record`, `f32 bounds[6]`. Grid handles are operands in the program's constant block.
4. **Units, domains, reporting.** σ in m⁻¹ using the scene's metres-per-unit; NEBULAR inputs in cgs (cm⁻³, K) converted at load. Every tabulated input declares its domain; outside it the rule is 0003 D8 (clamp to the edge value, listed once in the per-render report with the requested range). Wavelengths are vacuum wavelengths throughout (0003 D9; D33). Report entries raised by this record (codes, schema, deduplication and polling: 0001): domain clamps; Cubic rendered as Linear (D5); unsupported volume programs, lobes and phase options (D2, D7, D21); `majorant_violation` (D7); `VOLUME_LIMIT` and `STACK_OVERFLOW` counts (D8, D16); textured interior IOR approximated (D17); open medium meshes (D20); spatially varying world volume (D21); fluorescence and in-scattering treated as extinction in the curved region (D15, D29); gated-out delta-light contributions per light and light paths ended in the region (D28); excluded ergosphere volume (D30); omitted lines and the missing nebular continuum (D33, D34).
5. **Grids are NanoVDB** (Museth 2021; header-only Apache-2.0, vendored, admitted under 0001 D9), read in kernels with NanoVDB's accessor and `SampleFromVoxels`: order 1 for Blender's Linear, order 0 for Closest; Cubic is reported and rendered as Linear. **Grid format across the C ABI (ruling 11):** sparse 8³ leaves. One grid descriptor carries the grid name, channel count (1, or 3 for vec3 grids), background value, the index-to-object affine (3×4) and the object-to-world matrix, then n leaves: `i32 origin[n][3]` (index space, multiples of 8) and `f32 voxels[n][8][8][8][channels]`, indexed [x][y][z] with z fastest (NanoVDB's leaf order), channels interleaved. Leaves may arrive in several batches; struct and function names, and batching calls, are 0007's under 0001's naming rules. Native code builds the NanoVDB grid from the leaves with no OpenVDB; memory and transfer scale with active voxels. The addon gets voxels through Blender's bundled `openvdb` module: `openvdb.read` on the volume's file (after `VolumeGrids.save` for generated grids), then `copyToArray` over the active bounding box in 64³ chunks (1 MiB transient each); NumPy keeps the 8³ leaves holding any non-background value. Tests and the CLI produce the same leaf list from dense arrays with one helper. Spike G (Risk 5) gates building this route.
6. **Majorant grid** (pbrt-v4 §11.4.2 `MajorantGrid` / `DDAMajorantIterator`, written from the book): a uniform grid of at most 128³ cells, each covering 16³ voxels of the finest referenced grid, dilated one voxel for trilinear support, traversed by 3-D DDA. MEASURED (v1): cell edges 4, 8 and 16 gave no variance difference. Each cell stores σ̄ (an upper bound on σ_a+σ_s over the render domain) and j̄ (an upper bound on emission), 8 B, so at most 16 MiB per medium; NanoVDB grids and majorant grids are entries in 0007's VRAM budget table (ruling 19). Both bounds come from **interval evaluation of the volume program over the cell**; the interval of a grid lookup is the grid's min/max over the dilated cell, gathered from the leaves at build. One rule covers constant, grid and procedural media. Rebuilt when the program, a grid or the render domain changes.
7. **Interval rules (requirement on 0005).** Every opcode reachable from a volume program has an interval rule; the opcode table generator refuses to build without one. A program with no bound is reported unsupported and its volume is not rendered. A runtime counter `majorant_violation` counts samples with σ_t > σ̄ and is reported.

### B. Tracking, emission, phase

8. **Scattering media: null-scattering delta tracking with hero-wavelength spectral MIS**: event probabilities from the path's hero lane (0003 D6), pdfs at all lanes through `r_u`/`r_l` (0003 D7; pbrt-v4 `VolPathIntegrator::Li`, `wavefront/media.cpp`, with the hero lane in place of lane 0; Miller, Georgiev, Jarosz 2019; Woodcock et al. 1965; Wilkie et al. 2014). Shadow transmittance uses ratio tracking (Novák, Selle, Jarosz 2014) with the same rescaling. Homogeneous media run the same loop with one cell. Null collisions are not bounces. Limits per flight: 1,048,576 tentative collisions, 131,072 per shadow segment (PROVISIONAL, v1); beyond them the path ends with `VOLUME_LIMIT`, counted and reported. **Per-launch cap:** at most 4,096 tentative collisions per item per launch (PROVISIONAL, sized to keep launches inside 0009 B-17, ruling 14); then the item moves its origin to the current point (free flight is memoryless), shortens its segment and requeues, in `shade_volume` and in `shade_shadow` alike. A u32 `medium_rng` counter in the path's medium state (and in the shadow item) continues 0003's variable-count sampler stream, so a capped run is bit-identical to an uncapped one.
9. **Estimator per segment piece.** If any active stack medium (D17) has σ_s > 0 on the piece, all active media are delta-tracked together over the summed majorant, and every emissive one contributes β·j/σ̄ at each tentative collision (pbrt-v4 `r_e` form) with an emission floor σ̄ ≥ 8/diag(medium AABB) (PROVISIONAL, v1). Otherwise `march_emission_absorption(piece)`, shared by `shade_volume` and `advance_geodesic` (C1):
   - walks the majorant DDA and skips cells with σ̄ = 0 and j̄ = 0;
   - inside a cell, sub-steps have length ≤ min(half a voxel, the length at which σ̄·Δℓ = 0.1) (PROVISIONAL), with a per-ray random offset of the first sub-step;
   - coefficients at the sub-step midpoint; per sub-step the emission term S·(1 − e^(−Δτ)), S = j/α, is added through the running transmittance and the transmittance is multiplied by e^(−Δτ), with a 2nd-order Taylor branch for Δτ < 1e-3 (ipole `ipolarray.c`; v1);
   - at the end of the piece the throughput is multiplied by the piece's transmittance.
   **Accumulation (ruling 1).** The path state carries no radiance. Emission from either estimator is summed in a stage-local value multiplied by β and added to the per-sample buffer (0008) once per piece or launch. Its cost is unmeasured; V20 measures lookups per ray and milliseconds per 1080p frame.
10. **Volume NEE.** At a scatter vertex: the light sampler (0004), ratio-tracked shadows, MIS by the balance heuristic in r_u/r_l form (0004 D1, ruling 5). On camera and continuation segments, distance sampling combines delta tracking with equiangular sampling (Kulla and Fajardo 2012), 1/2 each, under the same balance heuristic. MEASURED (v1, CPU): smoke-plume variance 21.6x to 2.05x of a reference renderer; distance-only 3.45e4x. With the light tree (0004), segment light selection uses Conty Estevez and Kulla 2018 §5.3 Eq. 4 through 0004 D7's segment context. A lamp hit inside a bounded medium is weighted by Tr(lamp), never by path survival (v1: 17-20% dark).
11. **Light paths (0004 D18-D23).** Light-path items run the same tracking in importance mode, collecting no emission. At a real elastic scatter event `shade_volume` emits a camera-connection item (phase value toward the camera, ratio-tracked transmittance in the shadow stage) and terminates the light path, since a phase sample is never delta (0004 D19). At an inelastic event (D15) the light path ends without connecting: camera paths own those contributions (0004 D19, ruling 44). The connection is splatted with 0008's normalised pixel filter footprint, like camera samples (ruling 2; 0004 D23). Marched media multiply the light path's power by their transmittance. Light-path items carry the same 20 B medium state as camera paths (ruling 8).
12. **Phase functions, closed `PhaseKind`:** `HG` (Henyey and Greenstein 1941; exact sampling, weight 1), `RAYLEIGH`, `DRAINE` (Draine 2003; g, α), `MIE_JD` (Jendersie and d'Eon 2023 HG+Draine fit; droplet diameter), `FOURNIER_FORAND` (Fournier and Forand 1994; backscatter fraction B and IOR as Blender exposes them, clamped as Cycles `volume_fournier_forand.h`; the sampling routine is read from Cycles `kernel/closure/volume_util.h`, Apache-2.0, at implementation). A λ-dependent phase is sampled at the hero lane and evaluated at all lanes. Each kind has a quadrature normalisation test and a χ² test.
13. **Blackbody.** Planck B_λ(T) per wavelength in log form (0003 D25 owns the function): no overflow, no NaN at low T (v1's float normaliser gave NaN at 25-100 K). Blender's Blackbody Intensity is not clamped (v1: a [0,1] clamp made fire 3x dim).
14. **Volume bounces** follow Blender: `volume_bounces` counts scatter events with terminate-after (internal limit max + 1). Dropping the continuation is rejected (v1: smoke lit by embedded fire rendered black).
15. **Volumetric fluorescence** (owner decision 19, astrophysical case). A medium with `has_fluorescence` adds an inelastic coefficient through the reradiation file that 0005 item 26 shares: M(λi→λo) = Q·a(λi)·e(λo), or a 2-D bispectral table. At a real collision the inelastic event is chosen, at the hero lane, in proportion to its share of σ_s+σ_f; it terminates secondaries, samples λi from a for λt[0], the lane kept (ruling 24), and calls `rewrite_wavelength` (0003 D5), with isotropic re-emission; on a light path the event ends the path instead (D11). Dust photoluminescence, Bowen-type line pumping and X-ray Kα fluorescence are data (tables over the 0003 domain), not code. It needs delta tracking, so it is reported and treated as absorption inside the curved region. It shares 0005's reradiation file and follows 0003 D7's MIS convention after a rewrite.

### C. Medium stack and nested dielectrics

16. **One per-path stack serves media and nested dielectrics** (ruling 8): 4 entries (PROVISIONAL), each a u32 = material id (24 bits) + priority (8 bits), depth in 3 path-flag bits (0003 D21), plus the u32 `medium_rng`: 20 B per path, conditional, allocated when the scene has any medium or at least two distinct transmissive materials. Its place in the path-state budget is 0003's. With one transmissive material and no media, face orientation alone gives the IOR ratio. The world medium is the implicit bottom entry. A front-face crossing of a material with an interior (volume output or transmission) pushes; a back-face crossing removes that material's entry. Overflow ends the path with `STACK_OVERFLOW`, counted and reported. As implemented (`src/kernel/medium/medium_stack.h`): entry = id << 8 | priority, id >= 1 (0 is an empty slot), entries 0..depth-1 oldest first with the depth in path-flag bits 12-14; a push at depth 4 changes nothing and reports failure; a remove takes the most recent entry of that id and closes the gap. Whether the hit is a push or a remove comes from the face (front face enters), and a true interface is decided with that: entering, the hit's priority is at least every entry's; leaving, the hit's entry is itself the highest (greatest priority, latest among ties). A remove of an id not on the stack is refused (failure reported, stack and flags unchanged) and a back face whose id is not on the stack is a true interface, e.g. for a path that started inside the medium; the caller reports it once as a medium-path warning (lead ruling on task 180).
17. **Priorities** (Schmidt and Budge 2002). Visibility is derived, not stored: the **active media** on a piece are the volume-bearing entries at the highest priority present, all summed, so Blender's all-zero default sums every medium as Cycles does. At a crossing, the interface is true only if the hit material has the highest priority among the stack and itself (ties: most recently entered). A false interface is handled at the top of `shade_surface`, before the VM: update the stack, keep the direction, no bounce, requeue to intersect. At a true interface the IOR ratio is n(highest after) / n(highest before). The other side's n comes from that material's `interior_ior`, a constant spectral source in its constant block (0005 item 2a; ruling 28). A textured IOR contributes its constant fallback and is reported `approximated`. No second material is ever run through the VM. If this pushes the capped `shade_surface` build past its recorded spill or stack allowance (0002's gate, ruling 17), the lookup moves to the intersect routing step, which writes the resolved source into the hit record. `medium_priority` (u8, default 0) is 0005 item 2a's; 0007 exposes it. The interior medium of a translucent surface (`transmission_depth` > 0) is lowered by 0005 item 4 to this record's coefficient-level `MediumSample` (D2).
18. **Overlapping media.** Coefficients of the active media are summed; the majorant is the sum of their majorants; the interacting medium is chosen in proportion to its σ at the hero lane (Novák et al. 2018 §3-4). The estimator rule is D9.
19. **Shadow rays cross media.** When the scene has media, the shadow item carries the full 16 B stack and `medium_rng` instead of one medium id (layout and budget: 0003, ruling 9). Medium-boundary hulls are non-opaque to shadow rays; `shade_shadow`, over the hits 0002 D11 hands it in t order, ratio-tracks the active media on each interval up to t_max, updates the stack at each boundary with the D16-D17 code and requeues as 0002 D11 defines. Surfaces with a non-null BSDF still block (0004 D4). Delivered transmittance goes to the per-sample buffer through the shadow stage (ruling 1). If ratio tracking breaks `shade_shadow`'s gate, it splits under 0002 D9.
20. **Camera inside a medium.** The host computes the initial stack once per camera change by a point-in-closed-mesh parity test at the lens centre over medium-bearing meshes. Thin-lens apertures straddling a boundary are a documented limit. Medium-bearing meshes are checked for closed, consistent winding at sync; an open mesh renders with Cycles' convention and a report warning. Every medium-bearing mesh carries instance-mask bit 6 whatever its ray visibility, so every ray type updates the stack at it; a ray that hits it only through bit 6 treats it as a false interface (D17; 0002 D11). Volume objects get a 12-triangle hull of their active bounds with instance-mask bit 6, `MEDIUM_BOUNDARY` (0002 D11; bit 7 stays spare, ruling 43), and no surface shading.

### D. Blender volume shaders (VM `volume` entry ops)

21. **Mapping rules.**
   - **Principled Volume:** σ_s, σ_a per the Cycles mapping in `v1-lessons/research/volumes.md`. Colours are upsampled as reflectance before the per-wavelength formula, never after (the upsampling is nonlinear). Emission = strength × colour (illuminant) + blackbody term (D13), independent of density. Density, temperature and colour attributes are grid lookups.
   - **Volume Absorption, Volume Scatter, Volume Coefficients** use the same op family; each Volume Scatter phase option maps to a `PhaseKind` (D12) or is reported unsupported. The 5.3 enum is read from RNA at implementation.
   - **Mix and Add Shader** sum coefficients (C3).
   - **World volume** (World Output volume socket) is a homogeneous, **infinite** bottom-of-stack medium. An escaping segment has transmittance exp(−σ_t·∞): the environment and sun are invisible through an absorbing world medium, matching Blender's documented behaviour ("World Environment": the surface shader and sun have no effect when a volume shader is used). A homogeneous emissive world medium contributes j/σ_t analytically (to the per-sample buffer). j > 0 with σ_t = 0 at any lane is an error. A spatially varying world volume is reported unsupported.
   - **Blender velocity grids** are motion-blur data (0004/0007) and never drive Doppler shifts.
   Each formula is unit-tested against Cycles 5.3 `svm_node_principled_volume` source, never image-parity (owner decision 1).

### E. Astrophysics core: spacetime and geodesics

22. **One geodesic stage, `advance_geodesic`, a CUDA kernel that emits ray segments** (ruling 18; name, queue and resource gates: 0002). The CPU target runs the same kernel source. Segments go through the ordinary `intersect_closest` and acceleration structure; no material, medium, VM or policy code runs in an OptiX program. Every intersect result for an item with `IN_CURVED_REGION` (0003 D21), hit or miss, routes back to `advance_geodesic` (0002 D8, D11). Per item and launch the stage (ruling 46):
   - **resumes** a returned chord: march the active media over [0, t_hit or `ray_tmax`] (D9, D30), then handle the hit as D27 says;
   - **steps** (D26) as many times as it can while each new chord cannot touch scene geometry (tested against the scene bounds), marching the active media along each such chord in-kernel (D9, D30) and testing the outcomes (D27) after every step;
   - **emits** the first chord that overlaps the scene bounds: it writes the chord between `to_world` of the two states into `ray_o`, `ray_d` and its length into `ray_tmax`, the only copy of the length (0003 D29; ruling 25), and queues `intersect_closest`, whose region branch (0003 D28) traces with `ray_tmax` as tmax.
   Entering rays and camera rays inside R_c arrive at a step (0003 D28). Steps per launch are capped to keep launches inside 0009 B-17 (ruling 14); an item at the cap requeues itself. A ray takes 100-300 accepted steps (DERIVED: the 0.1·r step cap alone gives ln(r_max/r_min)/0.1 ≈ 80 steps from 22,000 M to 10 M), but only chords that overlap the scene bounds cost a wavefront round trip (Risk 7).
23. **Policy interface: 0003 D28's, unchanged in names.** The stage calls only `region`, `State`, `enter`, `rhs`, `hamiltonian`, `max_step`, `captured`, `escaped`, `to_world`, `redshift`, `tetrad`, plus one addition requested of 0003: `static_observer(s, u) -> bool` (false inside the ergosphere). The shipping kernel instantiates `KerrSchild` only; 0003's `FlatSpacetime` is instantiated in the CPU test build. Ordinary stages contain no policy code; 0003 D28's launch-uniform branch clamps `tmax` at the region sphere and routes entering rays and rays whose origin lies inside it. `KerrSchild`: Cartesian Kerr-Schild, G = c = M = 1, spin a ∈ [0, 0.998] (Thorne 1974). H = ½(η^{μν}p_μp_ν − f(l·p)²), g = η + f l l, f = 2r³/(r⁴ + a²z²) (Visser 2007; GRay2 eqs 2-4). `tetrad` uses Gram-Schmidt on the metric (KPolaris §2.1). No Boyer-Lindquist and no atlas: Kerr-Schild has no pole or horizon singularity (GRay2 §2.1, Blacklight §2.2). No Christoffel arrays, no virtuals, no float metric on a double state (v1).
24. **Launch.** `enter(o, d, time)` builds the momentum from the static-observer tetrad at o: the photon direction d, expressed in that local frame, gives the contravariant p^μ ∝ e₀^μ + Σ d_a e_a^μ. The result is null by construction and normalised so that the static observer at o measures unit frequency (0003 D28). The same call serves a ray entering at the R_c sphere (d = the flat world direction; the mismatch is the O(M/R_c) truncation of D25) and **a camera inside R_c**, the normal case for the target. The camera is at rest in the hole's static frame, because no camera velocity crosses the ABI (0007 decision 41), and is launched directly at t = 0. A camera inside the ergosphere is an error. The sign is the past-directed root: v1's future-directed root mirrored the shadow, and a unit test pins it.
25. **One hole per scene; the region.** The hole is an Astroray object (0007) with mass, spin, transform (spin axis = local +z) and `meters_per_rg`; a second hole is an error. The curved region is a sphere of radius R_c = max(50 M, 4M/θ_tol). θ_tol is half the angular size of one pixel at the **full output resolution** and field of view: the render resolution for final renders, the viewport region's full size in the viewport. The dynamic-resolution controller never changes it, so R_c is fixed within an epoch. Example: 1080p, 40°: θ_tol ≈ 1.8e-4, R_c ≈ 22,000 M; a 50-pixel shadow puts the camera near 290 M, inside. The weak-field deflection outside R_c is ≤ 4M/R_c ≤ θ_tol, and the frequency shift between R_c and infinity is ≤ M/R_c ≤ θ_tol/4 (DERIVED from 4M/b; research 06 F6). **Frames:** all static observers outside R_c are one frame, called "infinity"; the film measures frequencies in the camera's static frame. A science render may tighten θ_tol.
26. **Integrator.** Dormand-Prince RK5(4) with embedded error control (Dormand and Prince 1980), same source on both targets. Step factor 0.9·err^(−1/5), clamped to [0.2, 5] (Hairer, Nørsett, Wanner 1993). Mixed tolerance on (x, p): reference rtol 1e-9, atol 1e-11; viewport rtol 1e-5, atol 1e-7 (PROVISIONAL, S1). Steps are capped at 0.1·r so an equatorial crossing is not stepped over; chord sag ≤ 1.3e-3·r (DERIVED, L²/8R). At most 10,000 steps per ray (PROVISIONAL).
27. **Chords and outcomes.** Chords trace with closest hit (mask: surfaces | medium boundaries; Gröller 1995; v1 #1063). On resume: a medium-boundary hit updates the stack with the D16-D17 code (no VM) and the rest of the chord is emitted again; a surface hit ends the stage: the hit is on the chord, the incoming direction is the chord direction, the wavelengths are shifted to the local static frame by `apply_frequency_shift` (0003 D5), `LAST_SEGMENT_CURVED` is set, `IN_CURVED_REGION` cleared, and the hit is routed with `route_hit`'s table; a miss goes to the next step. Outcomes of a step, each distinct in diagnostics (0003 D22 names): `escaped(s)` (r > R_c, moving outward) returns the ray to the flat intersect queue from the **exit** point and direction after `apply_frequency_shift` to infinity (v1's entry-point respawn made an 8-10% dark ring); `CAPTURED` when r < r_cap = (r_+ + r_ph,pro)/2 (v1's rule, validated against GYOTO in BL coordinates; re-validated in Kerr-Schild by V11 before it may change); `GEODESIC_STEP_LIMIT`; `GEODESIC_NUMERICAL` on NaN or |H|/(p_t)² > 1e-6 (PROVISIONAL; Mandal et al. 2021). Step exhaustion is never "captured" (v1 did that).
28. **Connections near the hole.** A straight NEE connection between a vertex x_v and a light point x_l is allowed iff the segment stays outside R_c, or it stays outside r_gate = 10 M and its first-order deflection Δθ = (2M/b)·|x₂/√(x₂²+b²) − x₁/√(x₁²+b²)| ≤ ε_NEE. Here b is the hole's distance from the segment's line and x₁, x₂ are the endpoints' signed positions along it (DERIVED: the integral that gives 4M/b for an infinite line). ε_NEE = 1e-2 rad (PROVISIONAL; set by inspection on the target scene; science renders set it to θ_tol). The gate depends only on the two endpoints. A BSDF path whose last segment was curved therefore uses the same gate, and the straight NEE pdf from its last vertex (12 B stored in the geodesic record), in its emitter MIS weight, so the weights sum to 1 (Veach 1997). The accepted bias is O(ε_NEE), the same order as D29's shading-direction error. Gated-out delta lights lose that contribution, and the report counts it per light. This replaces 0003 D30's sphere rule. Light paths that enter R_c end with `LIGHT_PATH_IN_REGION`, counted and reported. Camera connections use the same gate; a curved camera segment disarms 0004 D19's caustic partition, so caustics gated out of the light-path side stay on the camera side (0004 D23).
29. **Surfaces and scattering inside the region.** Shading uses the chord direction in world coordinates; the error is O(M/r), accepted and documented. A scattering medium inside the region is marched with σ_s counted as extinction (in-scattering omitted), and the report says so.

### F. Redshift and transfer

30. **One redshift law, flat and curved.** g = ν_obs/ν_em = (−p·u_obs)/(−p·u_em) (Cunningham 1975; KPolaris eq. 5); in flat space with a moving emitter it is the special-relativistic Doppler factor. With D24's normalisation, the frequency any observer u measures relative to the launch observer is −p·u. Rules:
    - transport wavelengths are expressed in the static frame at the current vertex; λ_em = g·λ_obs;
    - the invariant is I_λ·λ⁵ (≡ I_ν/ν³; Rybicki and Lightman 1979 §1.5; Lindquist 1966): observed radiance = g⁵ × emitter-frame radiance, and Δτ is invariant;
    - the marcher uses j and α at λ_em with Δℓ_em = (−p·u_em)/(−p·u_static)·Δℓ_static, and adds g⁵·S·(1 − e^(−Δτ)). On a chord, p at a sub-step is interpolated linearly between the chord's end states in the geodesic record (PROVISIONAL; checked by V16 and V19).
    No emitter applies a D³ or g factor itself (v1 double-counted: D⁴ measured where D³ was expected). Inside the ergosphere static observers do not exist: NEBULAR media there are not evaluated, and the excluded volume is reported.
31. **Spectral samples ride on the ray.** Null geodesics are achromatic (KPolaris §2.4): all lanes share one trajectory and one g, and the hero lane is unchanged (ruling 4). Frequency changes go only through 0003's `apply_frequency_shift`, with its unit test.

### G. The emission nebula with a black hole

32. **The nebula is one medium with model NEBULAR**, whose program reads n_H, T_e, x and velocity from grids or constants into one `NEBULAR` op:
    - **Density source.** A Volume object with grids `density` (n_H in cm⁻³ after scale), optional `temperature` (T_e, default 10⁴ K per medium) and optional `ionization` (x = n(H⁺)/n_H, default 1); or a constant medium in a mesh (Strömgren oracle).
    - **Electron density.** n_e = x·n_H·(1 + y_He) with y_He = 0.1, He singly ionised where H is (Osterbrock and Ferland 2006 ch. 2). Heavy-element ionic abundances are per-medium constants.
    - **Lines.** j_l = (1/4π)·n_e·n_ion,l·ε_l(T_e, n_e)·φ_l(λ_em), computed linearly in n_e·n_ion, so neutral gas (x = 0) gives j = 0 exactly. The table lookup uses log₁₀ max(n_e, n_e,min), where n_e,min is the grid's lower edge. Below it ε is the low-density limit, which is physical, since collisional de-excitation vanishes. T_e outside the table follows D4 (clamp and report), but a lookup with j = 0 is never reported. φ_l is a unit-area Gaussian of width σ_l = (λ_l/c)·√(kT_e/m_l + v_turb²) (Rybicki and Lightman 1979); v_turb per medium, default 0. Lines are never broadened for rendering (v1 used 15 nm FWHM, 150x physical).
    - **Gas absorption** is zero under Case B for the Balmer lines (Osterbrock and Ferland 2006); other line absorption uses `TABLE` sources on σ_a.
    - **Dust** is extinction only: σ_a(λ) = n_H·(A_V/N_H)·(A_λ/A_V)/(2.5 log₁₀e), A_λ/A_V from Cardelli, Clayton, Mathis 1989 at R_V = 3.1, valid for x = 0.3-10 µm⁻¹ (0.1-3.33 µm); outside, D4 applies. A_V/N_H = 5.3e-22 mag cm² from N_H/E(B−V) = 5.8e21 cm⁻² mag⁻¹ (Bohlin, Savage, Drake 1978) and R_V = 3.1, times a per-medium depletion factor. `dust_extinction` (BSD-3) is the cross-check oracle.
    - σ_s = 0, so the medium is marched (D9) everywhere. **Velocity**: optional vec3 grid or constant (km/s, static frame); `has_velocity` gates the Doppler path.
33. **Line set and wavelengths.** Hα, Hβ, Hγ, [O III] 4959/5007, [N II] 6548/6584, [S II] 6716/6731. **The engine's λ is the vacuum wavelength** (0003 D9). Every line-sensitive input (line tables, narrow-band filters, response curves) declares air or vacuum, and air is converted at load (Morton 2000). The CIE tables are used as published: their air/vacuum offset is ≤ 0.23 nm in 360-830 nm, below their 1 nm sampling. The nebular continuum is not modelled; the report says so.
34. **Emissivity tables `nebular_lines`** (binary, little-endian, versioned): header `{magic "ARNL", u32 version, u32 n_lines, u32 n_T, u32 n_ne, f64 log₁₀T range, f64 log₁₀n_e range}`, per line `{u32 species, u32 ion stage, f64 vacuum λ in nm, f64 mass in amu}`, then `f32 log₁₀ ε[line][iT][ine]` in W m³. Grid log T 3.5-4.5 in 17 points, log n_e 0-6 in 13 (PROVISIONAL), bilinear in (log T, log n_e). **A real table ships in the repository** as a table we generate (0001 D8.1), built by `tools/build_nebular_lines.py`:
    - Balmer lines from published Case B fits (candidate: Draine 2011 ch. 14; the fit coefficients are unverified until the builder task checks them against the book);
    - forbidden lines from a five-level-atom solution using A-values and collision strengths taken from the primary papers, each value cited in the tool (candidate: Draine 2011 App. F).
    A value whose source cannot be cleared under 0001 D8 omits that line, and the report says so; a line is never faked. Higher-fidelity packs from Storey and Hummer 1995, CHIANTI or Cloudy ship only as 0001 D8.5 data packs. The synthetic analytic pack is a test fixture only.
35. **Building the target:** the hole (D25) + the NEBULAR medium + embedded stars as point or sphere lights with blackbody spectra (ionisation precomputed, not coupled at render time) + an environment star field. No accretion disk. Beyond the general medium machinery (stack, grids, marcher, rewritable wavelengths, diagnostics, spectral film), the target needs `advance_geodesic`, the NEBULAR op, line-aware sampling and science outputs.
36. **Line-aware wavelength sampling.** 0003's `lines` component of the sensor wavelength table (0003 D3) gets one Gaussian per line of width √(σ_l² + (λ_l·v_max/c)²). Strong-field emission is covered by the continuous component. The mixture pdf is evaluated at all lanes (unbiased). Light paths use the same table (ruling 7). Unmeasured (spike E).
37. **Science outputs** (formulas, buffers and limits: 0008): radiance in W m⁻² sr⁻¹ nm⁻¹; band images and photon counts; an optional spectral cube; line-centroid (velocity) maps; per-pixel geodesic statistics (termination, steps, maximum |H| drift, minimum r); FITS provenance keys this record supplies: M, a, `meters_per_rg`, R_c, θ_tol, ε_NEE, tolerances, line-table version and citations. Never denoised (spine §8). Always rendered with double geodesics (D40; on the GPU, 0002 D8's `advance_geodesic_f64`).

### H. Clouds

38. **VDB clouds render as ordinary grid media** with HG or `MIE_JD` phase. A spectral cloud material (θ×λ Mie table from miepython (MIT) or BHMIE, ice habits from Yang et al. 2013 (CC-BY-4.0), per-voxel liquid/ice blend) would add a tabulated `PhaseKind` and a `MediumDesc` field when it is built; nothing is reserved for it (G4).

### I. Precision

39. **CPU precision** (ruling 41). Geodesic integration (state, RHS, step control) uses explicit double on the CPU in every build. The full double CPU build (`Real = double`, 0002 D3) is a test reference and is not shipped; in it marcher accumulation and medium sampling on geodesic chords are double too, and flat media follow the spine's `Real` switch. Embree traversal stays float (0002 C3).
40. **GPU viewport geodesics** use float only if spike S1 shows, against the double CPU reference at a = 0.9, 60° inclination, 1080p, 40° FOV, camera at 300 M (PROVISIONAL thresholds):
    - capture classification differs on ≤ 0.1% of rays;
    - escape direction error ≤ 0.25 pixel for 99.9% of escaped rays, excluding rays within 0.5 pixel of the critical curve;
    - g error ≤ 1e-4 relative at the 99.9th percentile. Derivation: the viewport displays RGB and bands, and the narrowest viewport band is a 1 nm narrow-band filter (PROVISIONAL), so 1e-4 at Hα (0.066 nm) is ≤ 7% of its width. Thermal widths (σ ≈ 0.02 nm at 10⁴ K) matter only for cubes and centroid maps, which are science outputs and always double;
    - zero NaN.
    S1 measures three variants: float, double, and **mixed** (double only inside r < r_mix, default 10 M, PROVISIONAL, switching precision at the boundary; one stage templated on the scalar type, built as 0002 D8's `advance_geodesic` and `advance_geodesic_f64`). The viewport uses the cheapest variant that passes. Pure double was ESTIMATED far below 0009 B-14's floor (2M rays × 100 steps × 7 RHS evaluations at FP64's 1/64 rate). If only double passes, the controller lowers resolution (decision 10); this is an owner question.
41. **Geodesic record**: stage-owned SoA side buffer, allocated only when a hole exists, outside the path state (v1's +52% regression; research 06 R2): the states at both ends of the current chord (x[4], p[4] each) and the step size h in the stage's scalar; step count (u32); maximum |H| drift (f32); minimum r (stage scalar); the last scattering vertex (12 B, D28). Both chord ends are kept because resume (D22) and the marcher's redshift (D30) need them across the intersect round trip; the chord length is the path state's `ray_tmax`, not stored here (ruling 25). 164 B in double, 92 B in float, `static_assert`ed.

## Consequences

- **Easy.** Principled Volume, VDB smoke and fire, world fog, glass in water and overlapping media render through one medium stage and one lowering path. A new phase kind, node op or coefficient model is one entry plus tests. Astrophysical emitters (a thin disk, synchrotron) plug into `medium_eval` and the one redshift law. The astro target is an addition: one stage, one op, one table.
- **Hard, accepted.** Surfaces near the hole get straight NEE only within ε_NEE, and gated delta lights are lost (reported). Light tracing stops at the region. One hole per scene, no camera in the ergosphere, a static camera. Dust is extinction only, there is no nebular continuum, and volumetric fluorescence is flat-space only. Interval rules on every volume opcode (0005). Each geodesic chord that overlaps the scene bounds is a wavefront round trip through `intersect_closest`. Grid ingestion depends on Blender's bundled `openvdb` module. Every volume runs its VM program per evaluation.
- **Cost when unused.** No media and one transmissive material: no stack, one empty-queue check. No hole: no `advance_geodesic` launches, no geodesic record, one uniform branch in intersect (0003 D28). No velocity, nebular emission or fluorescence: one flag test. A grid medium: its NanoVDB leaves plus at most 16 MiB of majorant grid (0007's budget table). Build time: the Kerr-Schild header is in one TU (compile budget: 0009 B-05).

## What must exist from the first milestone, and what can wait

**Must exist from the first milestone, because retrofitting is expensive:**
- The 20 B medium state (4-entry stack + `medium_rng`) as a conditional path-state field with its 3 depth flag bits, the stack and `medium_rng` in the shadow item, and the same state in light-path items (D8, D11, D16, D19): adding them later changes the path-state and shadow-item layouts (0003) that every stage reads.
- False-interface handling at the top of `shade_surface`, with `interior_ior` and `medium_priority` in the material record (D17; 0005 item 2a): adding it later changes the fused kernel's recorded spill and stack allowances and 0005's record layout.
- Hero-lane delta and ratio tracking with `r_u`/`r_l` and the per-launch cap with requeue (D8): the MIS bookkeeping is shared with surfaces (0004 D1), and the requeue changes `shade_volume`'s and `shade_shadow`'s flow and the shadow item.
- The coefficient-level `MediumSample` with emission as j, and the interval-rule requirement in 0005's opcode table generator (D2, D7): retrofitting interval rules onto every opcode written in the meantime is expensive, and j fixes every medium emitter's units.
- Medium code compiled in the double CPU build (D39): the precision oracle; retrofitting double-cleanliness later touches every medium function.

**Can be added later without redesign:** sparse-leaf grid ingestion and NanoVDB (D5, after spike G); majorant grids from interval evaluation (D6); the marcher and the estimator rule (D9); equiangular + distance MIS and segment light selection (D10); media on light paths (D11); phase kinds beyond HG and the full Blender mapping (D12, D21); overlapping media (D18); host camera-stack initialisation and hulls for Volume objects (D20); volumetric fluorescence (D15); the geodesic stage and everything in E-G (the geodesic record is a side buffer and the flag bits it uses are 0003's); science outputs (D37); spike S1 and the precision decision (D40); the spectral cloud material; Kerr thin disk (Bardeen, Press, Teukolsky 1972), slim disk, ADAF, synchrotron; nebular continuum, dust scattering (Weingartner and Draine 2001), per-voxel abundances; curved NEE, moving cameras, slow light, multiple holes, ergosphere cameras, ray bundles (James et al. 2015); polarisation; GRMHD import; Gold et al. 2020 test problems.

## Risks and open items

1. **S1, float geodesics near the photon ring:** unquantified by any source. It decides D40 and the astro viewport cost. Run S1-S5 (research 06) before the geodesic stage is built.
2. **Capture rule in Kerr-Schild.** Validated only in BL. If backward rays stall near the horizon, the fallback (rule plus an inward-moving test at r < r_ph,min) needs a citation first.
3. **Spectral majorant tightness.** Domain-wide σ̄ bounds waste null collisions on strongly chromatic media. Measure on a chromatic VDB; decomposition tracking is the alternative.
4. **Line sampling variance** with Doppler and redshift: spike E must include a narrow-line nebula.
5. **Spike G, grid ingestion (gating the D5 route).** In Blender 5.3 on 256³ and 512³ sparse smoke and on the owner's clouds: `VolumeGrids.save` (generated grids), `openvdb.read`, chunked `copyToArray` with leaf selection, ABI transfer, NanoVDB build, upload. Pass: the scene's first frame meets 0007's first-frame gate (ruling 21). On failure, the remaining remedies need a dependency decision (native `.vdb` reading via OpenVDB, 0001 D9). The bundled module is not a stable Blender API; the 5.4 CI leg (0007) must load it.
6. **Line-table provenance.** The Draine 2011 fits and per-transition constants are unverified candidates. Whether constants that NIST ASD also compiles may be taken from the primary papers is an owner question.
7. **Geodesic round trips and registers.** Under ruling 46 only chords that overlap the scene bounds take an `advance_geodesic` + `intersect_closest` round trip; a scene whose geometry or medium hulls fill much of the region can still need up to 100-300 wavefront steps per pass against 0002 D7's about 26, with small launches in the tail; host overhead per step is unmeasured. S3 measures steps and pass time on the target scene. DP5(4) holds 7 stage vectors; in double with the marcher and the VM inlined, `advance_geodesic` may exceed its spill and stack allowance; remedy by measurement: restrict media in the region to programs without procedural ops, or split marching into its own stage (0002).
8. **Truncation at R_c and the NEE gate.** Both are approximations bounded by θ_tol and ε_NEE. S3 measures step count against R_c. ε_NEE's default needs visual inspection on the target scene.
9. **Dense clouds.** Hundreds to thousands of events per path at albedo near 1, τ 20-100; path guiding is the lever if the cloud showcase misses its time.
10. **Blender 5.3 Volume Scatter phase enum and Volume Coefficients semantics:** read from RNA at implementation.
11. **Requirements on other records:** all applied (0002 D8, D11; 0003; 0004 D19, D23; 0005; 0007 decision 41).
