# 0008 — Film, outputs, denoising, DLSS and viewport image quality

Status: proposed (2026-11-14), revised after review (2026-11-14), reconciled with RULINGS.md (2026-11-14)

## Context

The film is where the engine's physical quantities become something a person or an instrument reads. The spine fixes the frame: transport is in physical units and conversion to display RGB, sensor bands or photon counts happens only at the film (SPINE 3); each path carries film wavelengths separate from its rewritable transport wavelengths, and the wavelength distribution is data (SPINE 3); one observer everywhere (SPINE 3); the film keeps first and second moments, the full denoiser buffer set and statistics buffers (SPINE 4, CHECKPOINT-1 "Added after the owner's answers"); OIDN is the default denoiser and DLSS Ray Reconstruction (DLSS-RR) is an optional NVIDIA viewport mode loaded from the driver, never bundled, never applied to final or science output (SPINE 8).

Owner constraints that bind this record: a steady 30 updates/s at full resolution, 24 the floor, 60 a later goal, more samples per displayed update as the image settles, first frame comparable to Cycles, dynamic resolution reduction only below the floor (0000 items 9-10, 21-23); DLSS in the viewport if it can be built (item 11); heat maps of sample count, bounce count and similar statistics (item 15); scientific accuracy; short builds and fast tests; full breadth in the first release (item 7).

**Frame time** (spike B, `spikes/B-blender-end-to-end/RESULTS.md` 1.1-1.3). Blender's main loop adds about 6 ms of CPU time to every redraw, which caps redraws (0007 decision 33 owns that ceiling). The engine renders on its own thread and stream, decoupled from that loop (spike B 1.3, Present recommendation 4), so the 6 ms adds latency but does not come out of the engine's GPU time. The engine's time per presented frame is therefore **B(rate) = 1/rate − t_present**, where t_present is the present work that shares the GPU or the PCIe link (resolve to half floats, device-to-host copy, Blender's upload and compute unpack). Render, resolve, denoise or DLSS, and pack must fit in B. B and its parts are 0009 B-25 (frame), B-26 (OIDN share), B-27 (DLSS share) and B-28 (resolve share); t_present is measured (Risks 9).

**Challenges to the spine: none.** This record departs from research, not from the spine. It overrides `research/04-integrator-architecture.md` R2 and R12 (film fed by per-contribution atomic adds into a float32 running sum), because neither gives exact per-pixel second moments (decision 3). It keeps R9's A/B-split adaptive metric, which record 0004 D16 adopts; the film supplies its class-A sum (decision 2).

Three things have to be settled together. (1) Tests need exact per-pixel moments (Welch/Z tests, 1x1 analytic films, `research/09-test-validation.md` design requirement 4; record 0003's requirement of under 1e-6 relative accumulation error at 2²⁴ samples per pixel). (2) Every denoiser consumes RGB and wants guides free of avoidable noise (`research/10-denoise-upscale.md` 3, 9), while the engine samples four wavelengths per path. (3) Science outputs (bands, photon counts, data cubes, FITS) must stay raw and traceable while display products are denoised, upscaled and exposure-adjusted.

## Decision

### Film core

1. **One film, many channels, one write path.** The film is a set of device-resident planes described by a `FilmLayout` built once per render from the requested outputs. Each channel has a name, a kind and an offset. A channel nobody requested gets offset -1 and no memory. Kernels write only through `film_*` functions in one source file (`film_write.h`), compiled for CPU and GPU. Planes are structure-of-arrays, one plane per component, rows bottom-up (Blender's pixel order and FITS' order, so no flips on output). The film and the per-sample buffer (decision 3) are allocated once at the largest size the session can use (the region size for the viewport, the output size for final renders). Lower render resolutions use a sub-rectangle, so a resolution change costs a clear, not an allocation.

2. **Channel kinds and how each is written.**
   - `MOMENT`: fp64 sum S1 and fp64 sum of squares S2 per component, plus a shared u32 sample count n per pixel. When adaptive sampling is on, the channels its metric reads also get an fp64 class-A sum S1A per component (Cycles' parity split, record 0004 D16). Used for display colour and every science channel. Written only by the resolve (decision 3), never with atomics.
   - `ADD`: fp32 sum per component, mean only. Used for light-path passes, light groups, guides, deterministic AOVs and statistics. Written directly by the producing stage with fp32 atomic adds (several paths of one pixel can be live in a tail pass, and shadow deliveries arrive after their path ended), so its sums depend on summation order at float rounding.
   - `COUNT`: u32 per pixel (counters, histograms, max values), written with u32 atomic add or max.
   - `CRYPTO`: fixed (id, weight) slots per pixel (decision 11).
   - `SPLAT`: an fp32 per-pass plane folded into fp64 S1/S2 (and S1A) by the resolve, allocated only when record 0004's light-tracing pass runs (decision 4).

3. **Per-sample buffer and resolve: one writer per pixel, no atomics on moments** (ruling 1; 0004 D5 uses this mechanism and does not restate it). The path state carries no accumulated radiance.
   - **Buffer.** Each camera sample of the current pass owns one slot, indexed by its `sample_id` (0003 decision 19; ruling 37): `L[4]` fp32 (transport lane i, later weighted by film wavelength i) and a u32 `flags` word, 20 B per slot. There is one slot per pixel at the allocated film size (1080p 41 MB, 4K 166 MB); a pass never holds more samples than that (0004 D16's tail rule keeps k·u ≤ N_pix).
   - **Slot assignment.** A pass gives each of its u active pixels k samples (k = 1 outside 0004 D16's tail). The film owns the active-pixel list: u32 pixel indices (plane indices at the allocated film size), capacity the allocated pixel count, allocated under the ledger's film category, and its count u (`src/host/film/active_pixels.h`). The per-pass kernel `film_build_active` writes it at pass start; in M1a it lists every pixel of the render sub-rectangle in row order, and M4 adds adaptive compaction in this kernel, so the list's producer changes in one place. `generate_camera` only reads it (the resolve reads it from task 101 on; until then it maps j to a pixel without the list, `film_active_pixel`). Under compaction `film_build_active` also writes u to a device word that the host reads before the refill; u sizes the refill launch and is the u of 0004 D16's tail rule (k·u ≤ N_pix). `ActivePixels::plan()` is the M1a stand-in that computes u on the host: it gives the m-th sample (0 ≤ m < k) of the j-th listed pixel slot j·k + m. Within an epoch every active pixel has the same count n, so the slot's sample index is n + m. The path carries its `sample_id`, and every shadow item it emits carries the same value (path-state and shadow-item fields: decision 23). The active-pixel list stays readable by every stage until the pass drains, because 0003 decision 23 derives the pixel from it.
   - **Writes.** Every camera-path contribution (emission found by the path, a lamp crossing, a shadow delivery, including deliveries that arrive after the path has ended and its pool slot has been refilled) goes through one function, `sample_add(slot, Spec)`, an fp32 atomic add into the slot; this is the function 0002 previously called `film_add`. At path end the path stores its final flags (`SECONDARIES_TERMINATED` and an invalid bit, 0003 decisions 5 and 22) into the slot.
   - **Resolve.** After 0002's end-of-pass drain, the resolve kernel runs one thread per active pixel. For m = 0..k−1 in order, it reads slot j·k + m; skips and counts an invalid sample (decision 6); recomputes (λf, pf) from the sample key (0003 decision 2); applies 0003's `film_weights(flags, pf)` and the radiance-scale removal 1/s (0003 decision 10) in double; converts the sample to every `MOMENT` channel x (decision 7); adds x to S1, x² to S2 and, when n + m is even, x to S1A, with plain fp64 read-add-write; and increments n. It then folds the pixel's `SPLAT` value (decision 4) and clears the pixel's slots and splat entry. One writer per pixel on both targets, so the moments need no atomics and no fp64 atomic exists anywhere.
   - **Interrupted pass.** Only a change that starts a new epoch interrupts a pass (0007 decision 25). The interrupted pass is not resolved: its slots, its `SPLAT` plane and every channel written directly during it (`ADD`, `COUNT`, `CRYPTO`) are cleared with the rest of the film by the epoch reset, and late writes carrying the old epoch are dropped (0004 D25), so no partial sum survives.
   - **Reproducibility.** A slot's value depends on the order of its own fp32 atomic adds on the GPU (about 1e-6 relative, 0002 D12); the resolve's order is fixed. The CPU with one thread is bit-exact.
   - **Statistics of the moments.** Mean = S1/n. Variance of the mean = (S2 − S1²/n) / (n(n−1)) for n ≥ 2, evaluated in double (cancellation analysis: Chan, Golub and LeVeque 1983). All three Blender pixel filters are non-negative (decision 5), so every sample carries weight 1 and no weight plane exists. Under Owen-scrambled Sobol the per-pixel samples are not independent and this variance is conservative; statistical tests that need its exact value run 0003's independent sampler mode. fp64 meets record 0003's 2²⁴-sample requirement, which float32 does not (V2). The cost is measured (Risks 1).

4. **Light-tracing splats.** Contributions that land on an arbitrary pixel (record 0004's light-tracing pass for gratings and caustics) go to the `SPLAT` per-pass plane by fp32 atomic add. Each splat is weighted by the same pixel filter as camera samples, normalised to unit integral and evaluated over its whole footprint (pbrt-v4 `RGBFilm::AddSplat`): every pixel whose centre lies within the filter radius receives its weighted share, never a single chosen pixel (ruling 2). The camera film and the light image therefore estimate the same filtered image; with Box at 1 px a splat lands in exactly one pixel. A splat converts at the light path's own film wavelengths and pdf, recomputed from its key and the sensor wavelength table (ruling 7, 0003). The photon weight already contains 1/P (0004 D20), so the per-pass plane is one independent estimate of the light image. The resolve (decision 3) adds it and its square to fp64 S1/S2 over passes, adds it to S1A on even passes, and clears the plane. Combined mean = camera mean + light-image mean; combined variance = camera variance + light-image variance (independent estimators). Nothing is counted twice only because record 0004 D19 partitions path space between the two estimators; that is 0004's obligation and its D19 test checks it. Unlike the camera film, the light image correlates neighbouring pixels within the filter radius; its per-pixel batch variance over passes stays valid.

5. **Filter by importance sampling, not splatting.** Each camera sample draws its sub-pixel offset from the pixel filter and contributes to exactly one pixel with weight 1 (Ernst, Stamminger and Greiner 2006; pbrt-v4 `FilterSampler`). Filters: Box, Gaussian and Blackman-Harris, all non-negative; default Blackman-Harris at 1.5 px, matching Blender. One filter applies to the whole render, every channel of a sample included. The width-to-kernel mapping is copied from Cycles `intern/cycles/scene/film.cpp` (Apache-2.0) and checked against it by a test. Camera-film pixels stay statistically independent, which both the moment tests and every denoiser assume.

6. **Invalid samples are counted and fail loudly.** A sample with a NaN or Inf, or a negative spectral radiance at any film wavelength, in its slot is not written to any channel and does not increment n. It increments the `invalid` counter, appears in 0001's per-render report, and a nonzero count fails every test. Clamping (Blender's direct and indirect clamp, applied by 0004) biases the result. When any clamp is non-zero, science channels refuse to write and say why. The other film conditions raised to that report are the zero-Col pixel count (decision 10), Cryptomatte overflow (decision 11), denoiser or DLSS unavailability (decisions 16, 19) and refused science requests (decisions 7, 9); their schema, codes and deduplication are 0001's.

### Sensor model

7. **The film is a list of response channels (one mechanism for display, bands, instruments and cubes).** A response channel is a tabulated curve R_k(λ) (piecewise linear, nm, vacuum wavelengths) plus a unit, `ENERGY` or `PHOTON`. At resolve a sample converts as x_k = (1/N) Σ_i L(λ_i) R_k(λ_i) w_u(λ_i) / p(λ_i), with N = 4. w_u = 1 for energy and λ/(hc) for photons. p is the per-wavelength pdf after any secondary-wavelength termination (pbrt-v4 `SampledWavelengths` convention, applied through 0003's `film_weights`). The conversion is linear in L. Channel groups:
   - **Display** (always present): the CIE 1931 2° colour-matching functions (CIE 15:2004), then the 3x3 XYZ-to-working-space matrix for the file's working space (Rec.709, Rec.2020 or ACEScg). These are generated together with the white point and upsampling tables, as SPINE 3 requires. Display values are relative: they are divided by Y_w, the luminance of the spectrum that record 0003 assigns to a white Blender emitter of strength 1, so that emitter shows as working-space (1,1,1). The `film_exposure` multiplier (default 1.0) scales display and light-pass channels only, like Cycles' film exposure. Blender's colour-management exposure stays Blender's. Adaptive sampling's X, Y, Z means (0004 D16) are an exact linear map of the RGB means.
   - **Band**: any user curve (filter × QE × optics throughput), named, in energy or photon units, absolute SI. Presets ship as data files: Johnson-Cousins and SDSS-like bands taken from the SVO Filter Profile Service, plus NIR and UV boxes.
   - **Cube**: B box responses over uniform bins [e_b, e_b+1) of equal width Δλ, each divided by Δλ, so each bin reports bin-averaged spectral radiance (W m⁻² sr⁻¹ nm⁻¹) or photon radiance. Non-uniform bins are refused with a message pointing to band channels (decision 13). A cube is just a set of channels; no separate code path exists.

8. **Wavelength sampling coverage is checked at render start.** The film hands its active channels to record 0003's wavelength-CDF builder. The default is the visible pdf when only display is active. When band or cube channels are active, the CDF is a mixture of the visible pdf and the normalised sum of band responses, at 0003 decision 3's mixture weight. If any active channel has R_k(λ) > 0 where p(λ) = 0, rendering fails before the first sample.

9. **Photon counts and absolute calibration.** Photon channels report expected counts per pixel: N_k = t · G_pix · ⟨L_k,ph⟩, where t is the exposure time [s] and G_pix = A · ∫_pix cos θ dω is the pixel étendue [m² sr]. G_pix is computed exactly on the host: the pixel rectangle is split into two triangles and each triangle's solid angle comes from Van Oosterom and Strackee 1983, plus the cos θ factor. A is the collecting area: π(f/2N)² from the Blender camera when depth of field is on, otherwise an explicit `instrument.area_m2`. If photon channels are requested with no area, rendering fails. Photon channels require the Box filter at width 1 px, because counts must partition the flux. The filter is per render (decision 5), so display and every other channel are then Box 1 px too; a render with any other filter fails with that explanation rather than switching the filter. Within one pixel the film averages over the image-plane footprint rather than over cos θ dω. That error is second order in the pixel's angular size and is checked by the wide-angle S5 test (Verification). Science channels are never multiplied by `film_exposure`.

### Passes and AOVs for Blender

10. **Pass list and how each pass is produced.** Blender's standard pass names are used where they exist, so the compositor's Denoise, Cryptomatte and Vector Blur nodes work unchanged. Engine-only passes are prefixed `AR `. A pass exists only when the feature that produces it exists. A session's film follows the latched `ar_settings.passes` (task 117); a requested pass name outside what this build produces is not allocated, never fails the render, and raises `PASS_UNSUPPORTED` (0001 D2.11), one entry per name per latched settings change (ruling 74).
    - **Primary-hit AOVs** (`ADD`), written by the surface stage when depth = 0: Depth (linear view-space z), Position, Normal, UV, IndexOB, IndexMA, Mist (from the World mist settings), Vector, Alpha. Which of these accumulate over samples and which keep only sample 0 follows Cycles `intern/cycles/kernel/film/data_passes.h`, to be read before implementation and quoted in the code.
    - **Light-path passes** (`ADD`, display RGB): DiffDir, DiffInd, DiffCol, GlossDir, GlossInd, GlossCol, TransDir, TransInd, TransCol, VolumeDir, VolumeInd, Emit, Env, AO, Shadow Catcher. AO is listed but not designed: no record defines the rays that would feed it (Risks 13). Classification follows Cycles (`kernel/film/light_passes.h`): the split is fixed at the first scattering vertex; direct and indirect are separated by bounce count (`docs/v1-lessons/research/blender.md`, "Render passes").
      - At the first scattering vertex the path records its class (surface or volume) and, for a surface, the diffuse and glossy fractions per lane of the BSDF evaluation of the sampled direction, as Cycles' `pass_diffuse_weight`/`pass_glossy_weight`; the transmission share is the remainder. Every later contribution of the path is split by these fractions. A shadow item from the first vertex carries the diffuse and glossy fractions of its own light-sample evaluation (the split of f into diffuse, glossy and remainder parts that Cycles' `BsdfEval` provides: a requirement on record 0005, placed and gated by decision 22). Because the transmission part is computed as contribution minus the other two, the three parts sum exactly to the contribution whatever the fractions' precision.
      - Demodulation is defined in RGB, never per wavelength: the film stores the raw contributions RGB(L_cat,dir) and RGB(L_cat,ind), where RGB() is the display conversion of decision 7; `<cat>Col` accumulates the path's RGB guide tint (decision 15) times the category's albedo at the first scattering vertex, computed exactly like the decision-15 albedo (each colour slot's exact RGB projection); at resolve, `<cat>Dir` = mean raw / mean Col per RGB component (likewise Ind), Cycles' film-side division. Where a Col component is below 1e-8 (PROVISIONAL), that Dir/Ind component is 0 and the pixel is counted in the report as light that Dir·Col recombination cannot restore.
      - Because RGB() is linear in L, Combined = Σ raw light-path contributions + Emit + Env + Volume + light image holds exactly per sample, before clamping; Dir·Col + Ind·Col = raw wherever Col is non-zero.
    - **Light groups** (`ADD`, display RGB, one plane per Blender `lightgroup`). Every emitter carries a u8 group id (0 = none): lights, emissive meshes, world, volume emission and astrophysical emitters (emission-mechanism groups, `docs/v1-lessons/research/survey.md`). Shadow items carry the id of the light that produced them; emission hits use the id of the emitter hit. Σ groups + ungrouped = Combined, per sample. Limit: 255 groups, subject to the memory check in decision 21.
    - **Shader AOV** (`ADD`): written by the material VM's AOV-output opcode at the primary hit (record 0005).
    - **Cryptomatte** (decision 11).
    - **Denoising passes**: `Denoising Albedo`, `Denoising Normal`, `Denoising Depth` and `Noisy Image`, as defined in decision 15. `Noisy Image` is always written when a denoiser ran.
    - **Statistics passes** (decision 12): `AR Sample Count`, `AR Bounces`, `AR Rel Error`, `AR Invalid`, `AR Time`, `AR Rays`, and `AR Term <reason>` on request.
    - **Band passes**: one single-channel pass per band channel, so they also reach Blender's multilayer EXR. Cubes go only to FITS (decision 13).

11. **Cryptomatte**, following the Psyop spec v1.2.0 (Friedman and Jones 2015, BSD-3). The id is MurmurHash3_x86_32 with seed 0 of the object, material or asset name, computed by the addon and passed in 0007's instance and material descriptors (appendix), converted with `hash_to_float` (alShaders2, Apache-2.0); the smhasher test vector "hello" gives 0x248bfa47. Each pixel has `levels` slots per type (Blender's setting, default 6, rounded up to even), written as levels/2 RGBA passes of two (id, weight) pairs each, as Cycles does: `CryptoObject00..02` at the default. At depth 0 the surface stage adds sample weight to a slot, claimed by atomic compare-and-swap on the id word followed by an atomic add on the weight. This is v1's race-free wavefront scheme. A sample that finds every slot taken increments an overflow counter, which is reported, never dropped silently. The host sorts slots by weight, normalises them and writes the `CryptoObject`, `CryptoMaterial` and `CryptoAsset` passes as RGBA (id0, w0, id1, w1). The manifest is written through `RenderResult.stamp_data_add_field` under `cryptomatte/<7 hex digits of MurmurHash3(layer name)>/{name, hash, conversion, manifest}`, Cycles' layout (`research/07-blender-feature-surface.md` F6).

12. **Statistics and heat-map buffers** (owner item 15). The per-path counters live in the path state (SPINE 3; fields in decision 23). At termination the path-end stage adds them to these channels with u32 atomic adds or max (a tail pass can end several paths of one pixel in one launch); the sample count n is the resolve's (decision 3):

    | Channel | Kind | Definition | Default |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | sample count | `COUNT` | n per pixel (shared with `MOMENT`) | on |
    | bounces | `ADD` + `COUNT` max | mean and max scattering events per path (surface or real volume scatter, delta included, null collisions excluded) | on |
    | rel error | derived | for n ≥ 2: max over display channels c of sqrt(Var[mean_c]) / max(\|mean_c\|, 1e-6); the floor is PROVISIONAL. For n < 2 the pass stores -1 and the heat map shows a fixed "no data" colour | on |
    | invalid | `COUNT` | decision 6 | always |
    | time | `ADD` | per-path sum of per-stage `clock64()` deltas (CPU: `__rdtsc`), normalised to the image mean. A relative cost map, not wall time | on request |
    | rays | `ADD` | camera, bounce and shadow rays traced per sample | on request |
    | termination | `COUNT` × reasons | per-pixel histogram over 0003's termination enum (0003 decision 22), one plane per value | on request |

    - **Reset rule.** Statistics channels clear on a new epoch (0007 decision 25; ruling 37), not on DLSS mode's per-frame colour reset. In DLSS mode they therefore count over all frames since the last edit. The colour film holds n = 1 in DLSS mode, so the rel-error view is unavailable there and the UI says so.
    - **Display.** The engine maps a statistic through viridis (Smith and van der Walt 2015, CC0), or a fixed categorical palette for termination reasons, into a display-referred sRGB RGBA8 frame. Record 0007 (decision 40) draws that frame with its legend on the route its probe P4 picks, so the view transform never touches it. A scene-linear heat map would be distorted: the default transform is AgX-like (spike B 1.4: 0.066 shows as 99/255). Default ranges: sample count [0, max spp], bounces [0, max depth], rel error on a log scale [1e-3, 1] (PROVISIONAL).
    - Final renders write the raw statistic values (not colour-mapped) as passes and into the FITS dump.

### Science outputs and files

13. **FITS is the engine's only file format; Blender writes everything else.** Combined, passes and band passes go to Blender's `RenderResult`, so Blender writes the PNG or multilayer EXR. This record designs no EXR output of its own (v1: an EXR library update broke a test runner, `docs/v1-lessons/research/testing.md`); tinyexr is on 0001's list for 0007's texture reading and for writing (ruling 34). A hand-written FITS 4.0 writer in the host library writes a primary HDU and IMAGE extensions only, with no table HDUs:
    - **Science file** (final renders with band or cube channels): an empty primary HDU with provenance keywords (engine version and commit, seed, spp, sampler, observer, working space, filter, wavelength-pdf name, clamp settings, `DENOISED = F`). Then one IMAGE extension per band (`EXTNAME`, `BUNIT` such as `W m-2 sr-1` or `ph`, plus `EXPTIME` and `AREA` for counts). Then a cube extension of NAXIS3 = B with a linear spectral WCS (`CTYPE3 = 'WAVE'`, `CUNIT3 = 'nm'`, `CRPIX3/CRVAL3/CDELT3`; Greisen et al. 2006). Cubes have uniform bins only, because the `WAVE-TAB` form needs a binary-table HDU (decision 7). Each data extension has `VAR` (variance of the mean) and `NSAMP` companions. BITPIX -32 by default, -64 on request (tests). Spatial axes carry pixel coordinates plus camera intrinsics as keywords; celestial WCS is not written (Risks 8).
    - **Film dump** (command-line host, tests, benchmarks): every allocated channel's mean, variance and counters in the same layout. Test images, golden files and benchmark artefacts are FITS; PNG conversion for reports is a script.
    - The writer accepts only `FilmView` (decision 17); there is no overload for a denoised image.

13a. **What readback returns** (ruling 33; the functions and descriptors are 0007 decision 5 and its appendix). `ar_session_pass_count` and `ar_session_pass_info` enumerate the passes the `FilmLayout` allocated, in decision 10's order and names, then the band passes. `ar_session_read_pass` returns floats per component and pixel in bottom-up rows: the mean (S1/n of a `MOMENT` channel, or an `ADD` channel's mean), the variance of the mean (`MOMENT` only, decision 3) or the count (`COUNT`). Combined is the denoised `DisplayImage` with `DENOISED` set when a denoiser ran, and `Noisy Image` the film's (decision 17); Cryptomatte passes come sorted and normalised (decision 11); statistics passes are raw values (decision 12). `ar_session_read_counters` returns 0004 D29's per-pass counters; `ar_session_write_fits` writes this decision's science file or film dump.

14. **PSF convolution and detector noise are not designed**; nothing is reserved for them.

### Denoising

15. **Guide buffers and their exact definitions** (`ADD`, render resolution, linear, before tone mapping). There is one definition for every denoiser, as in Cycles, which feeds the same denoising passes to OIDN and DLSS (`research/10-denoise-upscale.md` 2). Guides are free of wavelength noise, not of Monte Carlo noise: like any `ADD` channel they average per-sample values, which vary with the sub-pixel offset (decision 5), the Mix Shader branch (selected stochastically, SPINE 5; a guide takes the selected branch's values, so its mean converges to the mix-weighted guide) and the delta lobe chosen at a smooth dielectric (reflection or transmission).
    - **Denoising surface**: the first hit at which the path samples a non-delta lobe. Delta reflections and transmissions before it are followed (OIDN's recommendation). The path keeps an RGB guide tint (decision 23), initialised to 1 and multiplied at each delta vertex before the denoising surface by the sampled delta lobe's RGB weight, computed by the albedo rule below. Albedo guides and `<cat>Col` are multiplied by it. Depth, motion and hit distance refer to the **primary** surface, because reprojection needs it.
    - **colour**: the `MOMENT` display mean (viewport DLSS mode: the current frame's single sample), working-space RGB. In the copy handed to a denoiser, negative components are clamped to 0, because neither OIDN nor DLSS documents negative input. The film and `Noisy Image` keep the negatives.
    - **diffuse albedo**: the layering-weighted reflectance of the diffuse and subsurface lobes. **specular albedo**: the directional albedo E(wo) of the specular, metal and coat lobes, taken from the same albedo tables that OpenPBR albedo scaling uses (record 0005), not from a separate approximation. **OIDN albedo** = diffuse + specular, clamped to [0,1]. All are computed in working-space RGB from each colour slot's exact RGB projection (the RGB input, or the scene-build projection of a spectral table under the working-space white), never by Monte Carlo over the four wavelengths. That keeps chromatic noise out of the guides and keeps them in the colour image's observer.
    - **normal**: world-space shading normal after bump and normal mapping, front-facing to the incoming ray, averaged and not renormalised.
    - **roughness**: OpenPBR `specular_roughness` (linear, 0-1) at the denoising surface; packed into the normal's w for DLSS.
    - **depth**: linear view-space z of the primary hit; the sky stores 65504.
    - **motion**: one internal backward motion buffer in pixels at render resolution (current position to previous-frame position of the primary surface point), camera plus rigid object motion, with jitter removed. The previous-frame camera and transforms are 0007 decision 15's: the frame − 1 sample in final renders (with position steps where deformation blur exists), the previously presented generation in the viewport, which is therefore rigid only. Blender's 4-channel `Vector` pass and DLSS's motion input are both views of this buffer; each API's sign convention is fixed in code with a test.
    - **specular hit distance** (DLSS only): world-space distance from the primary hit to the next hit along a first bounce that sampled a specular, metal or coat lobe; 65504 if it escaped. Behaviour for diffuse first bounces is taken from the DLSS-RR Integration Guide.
    - **matrices and metadata** (host side): per-frame world-to-view and view-to-clip matrices, jitter offset, frame index and a history-reset flag.

16. **One denoiser interface.** The host function is `denoise(DenoiserState&, const DenoiseInputs&, DisplayImage& out, DenoiseMode)`. `DenoiseInputs` holds read-only views of colour, the guides and the metadata. `DenoiseMode` is `FINAL` or `VIEWPORT`. Backends: `OIDN`, and `DLSS_RR` (`VIEWPORT` only). Rules:
    - **Off the first-frame path.** The `DenoiserState` is created on a host worker thread when the session starts (OIDN device creation, filter commit and weight loading; in DLSS mode NGX init and feature creation). Until it is ready the viewport presents undenoised frames and the status line says "denoiser loading"; the first frame never waits for it. A final render waits for it only before its denoise step. The state then persists across resets and resolution changes (v1 measured a cold re-initialisation cost on every frame, `docs/v1-lessons/research/testing.md`). Its device memory is reported to 0007's ledger under the denoiser category.
    - **DLSS failure.** If DLSS is unavailable or fails, the UI and the per-render report say why, and the viewport uses OIDN with a visible notice.
    - **OIDN failure.** If OIDN cannot be created or fails (for example no sm_120 support, Risks 3), the noisy image is shown or written with `denoised = false`, and the error appears in the UI and the report. There is no CPU-OIDN fallback for a GPU render.
    - The OptiX denoiser, NRD, FSR Ray Regeneration and XeSS are not built (Alternatives).

17. **Science data is never denoised, and the type system enforces it.** Film planes are reachable only through `FilmView` (read-only) or the `film_*` write functions; denoisers write only `DisplayImage`. No function converts a `DisplayImage` into a film channel or passes one to the FITS writer. A negative-compile test proves both. A denoised Combined is written to Blender with the raw result in `Noisy Image`, and every output that went through a denoiser carries `denoised = true` in its metadata. Band, cube, photon and statistics channels are never denoiser inputs or outputs.

18. **OIDN.** The library is 0001's own build with renamed modules at its single pinned version (0001 D9; ruling 12), loaded lazily on first denoise; this record pins no version. It runs on a CUDA device created on the engine's stream (`oidnNewCUDADevice`, buffers shared with no copy) for GPU renders, and on the CPU device for CPU renders.
    - **Final**: filter `RT`, `hdr = true`, `quality = high`, albedo and normal prefiltered with the `RT` filter in auxiliary mode, then `cleanAux = true`. `inputScale` is left to OIDN's automatic estimate.
    - **Viewport**: filter `RT`, `hdr = true`, `quality = balanced`, no prefilter, `cleanAux = false`, applied to the accumulated progressive mean from `start_sample` (default 1) on every presented frame once the state is ready. Denoise time counts in the frame time used by the resolution controller (decision 20). If balanced exceeds 0009 B-26, quality drops to `fast`. If `fast` still exceeds it, the viewport denoises only frames presented after 0009 B-37's at-rest delay without scene or camera change, and the UI says so.
    - Alpha is not denoised: it is copied from the film.
    - **Where the library is found** (task 176). `DenoiserConfig::library_dir` names the folder of `astroray_oidn.dll`; it is loaded with `LoadLibraryExW` and `LOAD_LIBRARY_SEARCH_DLL_LOAD_DIR | LOAD_LIBRARY_SEARCH_DEFAULT_DIRS`, so its sibling modules come from that folder and never from `PATH` or the current directory; entry points are resolved with `GetProcAddress` and no import library is linked (0001 V5). Development builds default to the dependency cache's `oidn-2.5.0/extracted/bin`, compiled in by `cmake/deps.cmake`; the release location belongs to packaging. An empty or relative folder, a missing module or entry point, an unsupported device (`oidnIsCUDADeviceSupported` false) and every `oidnGetDeviceError` fail the state with the module path and OIDN's text.
    - **Quality mapping** (task 176). `DenoiseMode::FINAL` → `OIDN_QUALITY_HIGH` on the main filter and both guide prefilters; `DenoiseMode::VIEWPORT` → `OIDN_QUALITY_BALANCED`. `fast` is not used until a B-26 measurement asks for it. Prefilters run in place on the guide buffers, as Cycles does. Creation also commits and runs both modes once on a 1 × 1 image, so weight and kernel loading happen on the worker thread (decision 16) and the first real denoise only sizes its buffers (128 × 128 FINAL on the 5070 Ti: 102 ms first denoise without this, 3.4 ms with it). The device memory is the drop in free device bytes around creation and around each reconfiguration (new size or guide set), recorded under `DENOISER`; the CPU device records none.

### DLSS Ray Reconstruction

19. **DLSS-RR route.**
    - **Loading.** NGX SDK headers (`nvsdk_ngx*.h` only) are fetched at configure time by the dependency script with a pinned SHA-256, never committed. The build uses the SDK's header-only loader (`NVSDK_NGX_HEADER_ONLY`), included only in `src/denoise/dlss/` (ruling 39); nothing is linked. The headers are on 0001's dependency list (ruling 34). No NVIDIA DLL ships in any artefact. At runtime the engine calls `NVSDK_NGX_CUDA_Init` through the project-ID path with Astroray's own project ID and a custom engine type, never Blender's application ID. It then queries `NVSDK_NGX_CUDA_GetFeatureRequirements` and the `SuperSamplingDenoising` availability parameter, and creates and evaluates the feature with `NGX_CUDA_CREATE_DLSSD_EXT` and `NGX_CUDA_EVALUATE_DLSSD_EXT` on the engine's CUDA context and stream (`research/10-denoise-upscale.md` 1-3). Requires an NVIDIA GPU and driver ≥ 620 (driver-bundled DLSS, Blender PR #165500); this record owns that floor. Anything else shows the reason and keeps OIDN.
    - **Inputs.** Feature flags `IsHDR | MVLowRes`, plus `AlphaUpscaling` when the film is transparent (Cycles `denoiser_dlss.cpp`). The guides are those of decision 15, packed from film planes into `CUtexObject`s by one pack kernel. Minimum input 128×96. The optional depth-of-field guide (presets E/F), which Blender never connected (research 10, PR #153077 comment 49), is not built unless the depth-of-field scene of V12 part B fails; if it fails and the guide cannot be supplied, DLSS mode with depth of field on shows a visible notice.
    - **Sampling contract in DLSS mode.** One independent sample per pixel per frame, with the colour film reset every frame (statistics follow decision 12). Sub-pixel jitter from Halton(2,3) with 64 phases (the guide asks for at least 32), reported to DLSS as the frame's jitter offset. Paths use 0003's independent sampler mode (ruling 3), because blue-noise dithering and per-pixel Sobol correlate samples, which the guide forbids. Adaptive sampling is off.
    - **Licence constraints** (NVIDIA RTX SDKs licence, 2024-03-14). No redistribution of SDK material, so headers are fetched rather than shipped (4(b), 4(e)). NVIDIA GPUs only (Supplement 1). NVIDIA attribution in the addon's About panel (7.1(b)). The commercial-release notice (Supplement 4) does not apply to a non-commercial release (0000 item 5). The GPL addon neither contains nor links DLSS; the Apache-2.0 engine calls a driver component, as Blender's Apache-2.0 `denoiser_dlss.cpp` does. This is an interpretation, not legal advice (owner question 1).
    - **At rest.** In DLSS mode a still view keeps receiving single-sample DLSS frames (Blender's behaviour). There is no automatic switch to progressive accumulation plus OIDN (owner question 2).

20. **Dynamic resolution and upscaling.** The controller, its levels, triggers and hysteresis belong to record 0007 (decision 34). What the film provides it:
    - **OIDN mode**: the film runs at a sub-rectangle (decision 1) and resets on a resolution step. OIDN runs at render resolution, and the presenter upscales bilinearly (OIDN does not upscale, Blender PR #151133).
    - **DLSS mode**: DLSS-RR does not support dynamic resolution, so the controller's levels map to the presets DLAA → Quality (0.66) → Balanced (0.58) → Performance (0.5) → Ultra Performance (0.33). Each step recreates the feature and resets history; V12 part A measures the recreate time per step for 0007's hysteresis. DLSS output is at viewport resolution.
    - Denoise and pack time are part of the measured frame time in both modes.

21. **Memory.** The `FilmLayout` reports its bytes per channel, and the per-sample buffer's, to 0007's VRAM ledger under the film category; whether a render fits is decided by 0007's single reserve rule and budget table (0007 decision 14; ruling 19). Film planes are never trimmed or spilled: a render that does not fit fails with the film's per-channel breakdown, and the engine never silently degrades a channel. Sizes at 1080p: display `MOMENT` about 110 MB (160 MB with the class-A sum); the per-sample buffer 41 MB (decision 3); each fp32 RGB pass 25 MB; an RGB `SPLAT` about 125 MB; a 64-bin cube with variance 2.1 GB.

22. **Where film work runs, and its register cost.** Guide writes, denoising-surface tracking, the guide-tint update and the light-pass fractions run inside `shade_surface` (and `shade_background` for sky values), where record 0002's stage table and front-load rule already place first-hit denoiser buffers. There is no separate guide stage: it would re-run the material VM at the primary hit, and spike A measured a separate parameter pass at about the cost of the whole fused shade (1.18 vs 1.27 ms per 2²⁰ paths). Values go to the film as soon as they are known; only the decision-23 fields carry across bounces. Moment accumulation adds nothing to the shading kernels' registers: it runs in the separate resolve kernel (decision 3). 0002's register gate (spills and stack in the shipping capped build against recorded allowances; ruling 17) is measured with every film runtime flag on, because a runtime flag frees no registers (0002's probe build). If the gate fails, 0002's fallback ladder applies in order; never a variant kernel. The kernels this record needs, which 0002 D8 lists, names and budgets: `film_resolve` (decision 3), `film_adaptive` (0004 D16), `film_present`, `film_prepare_denoise` and `film_pack_dlss` (decisions 15, 16, 19), and `film_heatmap` (decision 12).

23. **Path-state and shadow-item fields this record needs** (0003 owns the layout and its byte budget, ruling 9; the film asks for no others):
    - Path state, unconditional: `sample_id` u32, 4 B (decision 3; 0003 derives the pixel from it as the active-pixel list entry j = `sample_id` / k). It reads 0003's existing `bounces` and termination reason for decision 12.
    - Path state, conditional: `guide_tint` Real3, 12 B, when denoiser guides or light passes are on (decisions 10, 15); `pass_frac` diffuse and glossy fractions per lane, unorm16 × 4 × 2, 16 B, when light passes are on (decision 10); `stats_time` u32 and `stats_rays` u32, 4 B each, when that statistic is requested (decision 12); flag bits: AOV written (1 bit, with primary-hit AOVs, so they are written once across pass-throughs, decision 10), denoising surface found (1 bit, with guides), first-vertex class pending/surface/volume (2 bits, with light passes) and first bounce specular (1 bit, DLSS specular hit distance, decision 15); 0003 decision 21 allocates exactly these (ruling 42).
    - Shadow item: `sample_id` u32, 4 B, on camera-path items (decision 3; light-path connection items carry a film position instead, 0004); `light_group` u8, 1 B, when light groups are on (decision 10). `pass_cat` u8 (first-vertex class and direct/indirect) and `pass_frac` 16 B, when light passes are on (decision 10), are not item fields: 0003 decision 20 keeps them in the shadow-pass side buffer, read by shadow slot.

## Alternatives considered

- **Atomic per-contribution film adds** (`research/04-integrator-architecture.md` R2; Cycles). No per-pixel second moment, so no Welch or Z tests (`research/09-test-validation.md` 1.4). Rejected for moments, kept for `ADD` channels that need only a mean. The A/B split of R9 is kept as 0004 D16's adaptive metric.
- **A per-path accumulator in the path state, written at path end** (this record's previous draft). A path may end and its pool slot be refilled while its shadow items are in flight, so their results are lost or misrouted (0004 Alternatives; ruling 1). The per-sample buffer has no such hazard.
- **fp64 atomic adds for moments** (this record's first draft). Unneeded with one writer per pixel in the resolve, and record 0002 provides no fp64 atomic.
- **A float32 running sum** (04 R12). It fails record 0003's 2²⁴-sample accuracy requirement, and S2 − S1²/n cancels badly in float (Chan et al. 1983). Kahan-compensated float uses the same memory as fp64 with more operations.
- **Splatting each camera sample through the filter footprint.** This correlates neighbouring pixels, which invalidates per-pixel variance and the independence that OIDN and DLSS assume. Filter importance sampling gives the same expected image (Ernst et al. 2006).
- **One stochastically chosen pixel per light-tracing splat** (0004's draft D23). Fewer atomics, but ruling 2 fixes the footprint form so that both estimators share one filter definition.
- **A full spectrum per pixel, converted at the end.** 64 bins at 1080p is 531 MB in fp32 (`docs/v1-lessons/research/survey.md`), and it still bins the spectrum. Converting at resolve is exact for any response (pbrt-v4 `RGBFilm::AddSample`). The cube remains as an opt-in set of box responses.
- **Per-wavelength demodulation of light-path passes.** The RGB projection of L/a times the RGB projection of a is not the RGB projection of L, so Dir·Col would not recombine to Combined. Division happens in RGB at resolve (decision 10).
- **A separate primary-hit guide stage.** Re-runs the VM at the primary hit (spike A: a separate parameter pass costs about as much as the whole fused shade). Rejected unless 0002's fallback ladder reaches it by measurement.
- **An engine-side EXR writer, or CFITSIO.** Blender already writes EXR from passes, and an EXR dependency is the failure v1 recorded. CFITSIO is rejected for build cost and dependency count, given that the writer emits only IMAGE HDUs; its licence is not the reason (permissive by its own text, to be confirmed in research 11 if it is ever adopted).
- **Non-uniform cube bins through `WAVE-TAB`.** Needs a binary-table HDU in the writer. Arbitrary spectral sampling is already served by band channels.
- **The OptiX denoiser as a second backend now.** Not measured to beat OIDN on this card (10, recommendation 5). v1's backend selector produced byte-identical output (`docs/v1-lessons/research/misc.md`), the classic failure of an untested second backend. It is added only if a measurement shows a win.
- **NRD, FSR Ray Regeneration, XeSS, or our own temporal accumulator.** NRD needs a D3D12 or Vulkan host and split radiance with hit-distance packing. FSR RR is DX12 and RDNA 4 only. XeSS is an upscaler, not a denoiser. An in-house temporal filter would be tuned by us and lose to trained models (10, sections 7-8 and options 5-6).
- **Bundling `nvngx_dlssd.dll`.** Licence 4(e), conflicting NDA wording in the Ray Reconstruction guide, and Blender's own resolution, driver-bundled DLSS from driver 620 (10, section 4).
- **Monte Carlo projection of the albedo guide over the four hero wavelengths.** It puts chromatic noise into the guides; the DLSS guide warns that noisy guides cause grain and ghosting.
- **Engine-side tonemapping for display.** Blender applies the view transform after `view_draw` (spike B 1.4), so the transform would be applied twice. Heat maps are the exception, drawn outside the transform (decision 12).
- **A CPU-OIDN fallback when the GPU OIDN device fails.** Hides a broken install behind a frame-rate collapse. The noisy image plus an error is shown instead (decision 16).
- **A hybrid viewport: DLSS while moving, OIDN progressive at rest.** It converges, but the image changes character under the user, and a Blender maintainer rejected it for that reason (10, section 2). Not built by default; left to the owner.

## Consequences

- **Easy.** A new band, instrument or cube is a data table, not code. A new pass is a channel descriptor plus one `film_*` call. Tests get exact means and variances from any render, CPU or GPU. Shadow results can never be lost to path lifetime. Denoised data cannot reach a science file by construction. Light groups by emission mechanism cost nothing extra for astrophysics.
- **Hard.** Moments cost fp64 planes (about 110 MB at 1080p), a 20 B-per-pixel per-sample buffer and one resolve launch per pass (Risks 1). The camera film is reproducible only to the summation order within a slot on the GPU. Light-path passes require the BSDF evaluation at the first vertex to return category parts, which adds register pressure in `shade_surface` (decision 22). DLSS mode constrains the sampler (independent mode, per-frame reset), so the viewport has two sampling regimes to test. Photon counts restrict the whole render to Box at 1 px. The light image is spatially correlated within the filter radius. Guides at 1 spp carry Mix Shader and sub-pixel noise.
- **Cost when unused.** An unrequested channel has no memory, and its write is one branch on a constant offset. Light-pass fields and the guide tint exist only when needed. With DLSS off, NGX is never loaded and the pack kernel never launched. With no splats there is no `SPLAT` plane and no splat atomics. With no science channels, the FITS writer is never called. The always-on cost is the display `MOMENT` channel, the per-sample buffer, the 4 B `sample_id`, the sample count and the invalid counter.

## What must exist from the start, and what can come later

Milestones are `docs/ROADMAP.md`'s (ruling G1).

**Must exist from the first milestone, because retrofitting is expensive:**
- The per-sample buffer, `sample_add` and the resolve into fp64 moments with S1A (decisions 2-3): they fix the path-state layout (a slot, no radiance), the shadow item, every contribution site and a kernel in 0002's list, and every statistical test depends on exact moments.
- `FilmLayout`, the single `film_*` write path and response-channel conversion with the generated observer (decisions 1, 7): every later pass, band and cube is a descriptor on them; an RGB shortcut for display would have to be torn out of every write site.
- Filter importance sampling (decision 5): it fixes camera-ray generation and the pixel independence that moment tests and denoisers are calibrated on.
- The `SPLAT` plane and its fold in the resolve (decision 4): the light-tracing pass is designed in from the start (owner item 17), and the resolve's inputs include it.
- The decision-23 fields in 0003's layout, allocated only when enabled: adding them later changes the path-state and shadow-item layout and 0003's budget.
- Guide writes, the guide tint and the denoising-surface bit inside `shade_surface`/`shade_background`, under 0002's register gate with every film flag on, including the DLSS-only specular hit distance if V12 part A passes (decision 22): 0002 front-loads register-heavy parts, and adding them later can break the gate.
- Invalid-sample counting and the statistics channels (decisions 6, 12): SPINE 3 puts the counters in the state, and every test fails on invalid samples.
- The denoiser interface with asynchronous creation, and the `FilmView`/`DisplayImage` split (decisions 16-17): later backends slot in without a signature change, and science isolation holds by construction only if no writer predates it.
- FITS film dumps (decision 13): every test image and benchmark artefact uses them; a format change later rewrites every reference and harness.
- Film byte reporting to 0007's ledger (decision 21).

**Can be added later without redesign:**
- Passes beyond Combined, Depth, Normal, Alpha and the denoising passes: Position, UV, IndexOB, IndexMA, Mist, Vector, light-path passes, Volume, Emit, Env, AO, Shadow Catcher, Cryptomatte, Shader AOV, light groups (channel descriptors on the existing path; their state fields already exist).
- Transparent film.
- The DLSS-RR backend: pack kernel, Halton jitter, preset mapping and the controller interplay of decision 20, behind decision 16's interface and subject to V12.
- Viewport OIDN quality stepping (decision 18).
- Band, photon and cube channels, instrument areas and exposure times, the science FITS file, false-colour band views.
- Celestial WCS, per-light-group science channels, and the OptiX denoiser or a temporal OIDN if measured to win.

## Verification

- **V1 Film normalisation.** Analytic scenes S1 (Lambertian sphere in a constant environment) and S5 (uniform emitter filling the view, pinhole and thin lens, wide field of view) from `research/09-test-validation.md`. Pass: |mean − truth| < 3 SE + 1e-4 per channel, CPU and GPU.
- **V2 Moments and the per-sample buffer.** Through `sample_add` and the resolve on a 1x1 film, CPU and GPU: 3e7 samples of 1.0 give S1 = 3e7 exactly (float32 stalls at 2²⁴ = 16,777,216), and 2²⁴ samples of 0.1 give S1 within 1e-6 relative of 2²⁴·0.1 (record 0003's requirement; float32 misses it by orders of magnitude). Synthetic iid samples with known variance (uniform, exponential) give a variance estimate inside its 99.9% interval. A tail pass with k > 1 gives the same moments as k single-sample passes. A scene whose shadow items are delivered after their paths ended, with a deliberately small main pool, gives the same film as a large-pool run within 1e-6 relative (no contribution lost or misrouted). CPU with one thread twice gives bitwise-identical camera film; GPU twice agrees within 1e-6 relative; CPU and GPU dumps agree bitwise in n and to float tolerance in S1 (FMA-off test build, as in spike A).
- **V3 Filter.** A χ² test of importance-sampled offsets against each filter, plus the width mapping compared with Cycles' filter table. A point splat's normalised footprint weights sum to 1 for each filter.
- **V4 Observer.** For each working space, a white reflector under the working-space illuminant resolves to (1,1,1) within 1e-4, and the CMFs integrate to Y (09 pure-function family).
- **V5 Sensor and science.** S10 blackbody: band radiance against Planck and photon counts against the analytic count, which fails on a 1e9 unit slip. Cube bins summed over Δλ equal the band integral within 3 SE. Astropy (BSD-3, test-only) reads the FITS file, and the WCS gives bin centres to 1e-9 nm. Photon-count requests with a non-Box filter or no area, and cubes with non-uniform bins, are refused.
- **V6 Passes.** At 1 spp with a fixed seed, per pixel to float tolerance: Combined = Σ raw light-path contributions + Emit + Env + Volume + light image; Dir·Col + Ind·Col = raw wherever Col is non-zero, and the zero-Col count in the report matches a scene built to contain such pixels; Combined = Σ light groups + ungrouped. Deterministic AOVs (Depth, Normal, UV, IndexOB) match between CPU and GPU (09 agreement ladder step 3).
- **V7 Cryptomatte.** Headless Blender 5.3: the compositor Cryptomatte node, given our manifest and levels/2 layers, extracts a known object's matte with mean coverage error ≤ 1e-3, and the overflow count is 0 on the test scene.
- **V8 Statistics.** With adaptive sampling off, the sample-count map equals the spp exactly. On a closed furnace with albedo ρ, no Russian roulette and max depth D, the mean bounce count equals the geometric expectation within 3 SE. A test material that injects a NaN makes `invalid` > 0 and fails the render report. Rel error is -1 where n < 2. In DLSS mode the sample-count map counts frames since the last edit.
- **V9 Science isolation.** A negative-compile test shows `DisplayImage` cannot be passed to `film_*` or the FITS writer. Every FITS file has `DENOISED = F`. A render with clamping and a band channel errors.
- **V10 Guides.** On a fronto-parallel constant-colour plane at 1 spp, the albedo and normal guides are bitwise identical between two seeds (no wavelength noise). On a mirror in front of a diffuse wall, the albedo follows the mirror, tinted by the guide tint. Per-pixel guide variance on a textured plane, a fractional Mix Shader and smooth glass is reported (not gated) and feeds V12 part B.
- **V11 OIDN.** Final: denoised relMSE against a 16k-spp reference is lower than the raw relMSE at 64 spp on three scenes. Viewport: balanced and fast are timed at 1080p and 4K on the 5070 Ti against 0009 B-26. Cold init (device creation, filter commit, weights) is timed; time to first frame with the denoiser on equals denoiser off within run-to-run noise (the first-frame gate itself is 0007 decision 35). A forced device-creation failure shows the noisy image, the error and `denoised = false`. The OIDN CUDA device runs on sm_120 (Risks 3).
- **V12 DLSS spike F.** Run on demand; nothing is scheduled.
  - **Part A** (synthetic colour, guides and motion; command-line host, then inside Blender 5.3 through ctypes; driver ≥ 620) runs before `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` is signed off, as CHECKPOINT-1 requires of spike F. All must pass for DLSS to ship as an option; if it fails, the DLSS-only items (specular hit distance, Halton jitter, the pack kernel, preset mapping) are not built, and the OIDN guides and motion stay:
    - (a) `GetFeatureRequirements` reports supported with no DLL beside either executable;
    - (b) project-ID init works without Blender's ID;
    - (c) pack plus evaluate, per preset, at 1080p and 4K output: at least one preset meets 0009 B-27;
    - (d) NGX-reported memory for each preset and output size is within 5 % of the guide's figure for that preset where the guide gives one, and every preset's figure is recorded for 0007 d14's VRAM ledger (the guide's "154-579 MB" is its Performance-mode figure at 1080p and 4K, not a range covering every preset; ruling 64);
    - (e) recorded, not gated: cold init time (decision 16) and feature recreate time per preset step (decision 20).
  - **Part B**, once the guides and the viewport loop exist, on five scenes (diffuse Cornell, glossy, emission nebula, black-hole lensing, thin-lens depth of field) against 16k-spp references. DLSS is on by default on NVIDIA only if part B also passes:
    - (a) relMSE during a scripted 5 s orbit is no worse than OIDN-balanced progressive at the same update rate on at least 3 of the first 4 scenes;
    - (b) no ghosting or highlight clamping judged by visual inspection of the saved frames, especially lensed regions;
    - (c) report chroma relMSE separately; it must not exceed OIDN's by more than 1.5× (PROVISIONAL);
    - (d) the independent sampler mode shows no structured artefacts against Owen-Sobol per frame;
    - (e) the depth-of-field scene meets (a)'s rule against OIDN, or decision 19's notice applies.
  - All timings use burn-in plus min-of-N (GPU clock drift).
  - **Part A result (spike F, ruling 64; evidence `docs/spikes/F-dlss/RESULTS.md`):** (a)-(d) pass, so part A passes and the DLSS-only items are built. (d): Performance measured 153.98 MB at 1080p and 596.7 MB at 4K (+3 %); DLAA at 4K needs about 1.45 GB, which 0007 d14's reserve rule accounts for. The CUDA context must exist before NGX init, and a preset-step recreate takes up to about 150 ms (input to 0007's hysteresis, decision 20).
- **V13 Build and test budgets.** OIDN (0001's release-asset build) and the NGX headers add nothing measurable to 0009's incremental-build budget (B-02). Film and denoiser GPU tests run in `check gate` (0009 decision 11), not in `check fast`; V11 timing and V12 part B run in `check full` or on demand.

## Risks and open items

1. **Resolve and buffer cost.** Measure resolve time and `sample_add` traffic at 1080p and 4K, 1 spp per pass; the first measurement sets 0009 B-28. The fp64 work no longer sits in a shading kernel, so it does not touch the register gate.
2. **Register cost of guides and the light-pass split** in `shade_surface`, unmeasured (spike A excluded denoiser buffers). Decision 22 places and gates it.
3. **OIDN on Blackwell.** Confirmed on sm_120 (task 176): our OIDN 2.5.0 build, NVIDIA driver 621.24, RTX 5070 Ti; `oidnIsCUDADeviceSupported(0)` is true, the CUDA device on the engine's stream creates and denoises, and on the 128 × 128 test fixture the relMSE ratio (denoised / raw) is 0.0323 FINAL and 0.0494 VIEWPORT, against 0.0323 and 0.0493 on the CPU device. Failure handling remains decision 16. DLL collisions with Blender's in-process OIDN are removed by 0001's renamed build.
4. **DLSS availability to a third-party process** without a local DLL on driver 620 was unverified (10, Unverified); V12 part A (a) passed (ruling 64). Hero-wavelength chroma noise, gravitational-lensing reprojection and depth of field under DLSS are hypotheses, tested in part B.
5. **Cryptomatte acceptance** of a Python engine's manifest by Blender's compositor is unverified (07); V7 decides.
6. **Denoising-surface definition.** Pure "first non-delta lobe" may smear near-mirror roughness. If V11 or V12 shows it, add a roughness threshold as Cycles does, measured.
7. **Data-cube memory** (2.1 GB for 64 bins at 1080p with variance) limits cube size on 16 GB. Tiling is not designed.
8. **Celestial WCS** (RA/Dec) needs a camera-to-sky convention that no record yet owns.
9. **Frame-time terms not yet measured.** t_present (the engine's device-to-host copy and Blender's unpack on the GPU) and Blender's own GPU drawing with overlays on (spike B ran with overlays off, Present recommendation 5). The redraw cap is a separate ceiling owned by 0007 (decision 33).
10. **Mix Shader guides at 1 spp.** Stochastic selection (SPINE 5) makes guides noisy where a mix factor is fractional, where Cycles mixes closures deterministically. V10 reports it and V12 part B tests it; if it causes ghosting, evaluating both branches' albedo is a question for record 0005.
